GST Return Filing Due Dates 2026-27: Complete Calendar

Published on October 6, 2026 • 14 min read • Updated for FY 2026-27

Missing a GST return filing deadline is one of the most common and costly compliance errors Indian businesses make. Late filing triggers automatic late fees starting from Rs 20 to Rs 50 per day, interest charges of 18% per annum on unpaid tax, and can even lead to blocking of your e-way bill generation facility. For businesses that depend on timely logistics, a missed deadline can bring operations to a halt.

This comprehensive calendar covers every GST return filing due date for Financial Year 2026-27 (April 2026 to March 2027). We cover GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, CMP-08, GSTR-4, GSTR-9, and all other return types — along with late fee calculations, interest rates, and strategies to avoid missing deadlines.

GSTR-1 Due Dates for FY 2026-27

GSTR-1 is the statement of outward supplies (sales) that every regular GST taxpayer must file. It contains invoice-level details of all B2B and B2C transactions. The due dates depend on whether you are a monthly filer or a quarterly filer under the QRMP scheme.

Businesses with an aggregate turnover exceeding Rs 5 crore in the preceding financial year must file GSTR-1 on a monthly basis. The due date is the 11th of the following month.

GSTR-4 Annual Return

Composition scheme taxpayers must file GSTR-4, the annual return, by April 30 of the year following the financial year. For FY 2026-27, the due date is April 30, 2028.

GSTR-4 consolidates the quarterly CMP-08 data and provides an annual summary of turnover, tax paid, and any adjustments. It also includes details of inward supplies attracting reverse charge.

Other GST Return Due Dates for FY 2026-27

GSTR-5: Non-Resident Taxable Persons (Due: 20th of Following Month)

Non-resident taxable persons who have registered in India for occasional supply of goods or services must file GSTR-5 monthly. The return covers outward supplies, inward supplies, tax paid, and refund claims. The due date is the 20th of the following month, or within 7 days after the last day of the validity period of registration, whichever is earlier.

GSTR-5A: OIDAR Service Providers (Due: 20th of Following Month)

Non-resident providers of Online Information and Database Access or Retrieval (OIDAR) services to non-taxable online recipients in India must file GSTR-5A by the 20th of the following month. OIDAR services include streaming platforms, cloud computing, digital advertising, and online gaming services provided from outside India.

GSTR-6: Input Service Distributors (Due: 13th of Following Month)

Input Service Distributors (ISD) must file GSTR-6 monthly by the 13th of the following month. This return contains details of input tax credit received and distributed to the recipient branches or units. The ISD mechanism is being reformed from April 2025 onwards, with mandatory ISD registration for entities that were previously distributing credit through cross-charge invoices.

GSTR-8: E-Commerce TCS Collectors (Due: 10th of Following Month)

E-commerce operators who collect Tax Collected at Source (TCS) under Section 52 must file GSTR-8 by the 10th of the following month. This return contains details of supplies made through the e-commerce platform, the amount of TCS collected, and TCS deposited.

GSTR-11: UIN Holders (Due: 28th of Following Month)

Persons with a Unique Identity Number (UIN), such as UN bodies and foreign diplomatic missions, must file GSTR-11 by the 28th of the month following the month of the inward supply for which they are claiming a refund of taxes paid.

Annual Return Due Dates

GSTR-9: Annual Return

GSTR-9 is the annual return that consolidates all monthly/quarterly returns filed during the financial year. It provides a complete summary of outward and inward supplies, tax paid, ITC claimed, and demands and refunds.

Financial YearGSTR-9 Due DateWho Must File
FY 2025-26December 31, 2026Regular taxpayers with turnover > Rs 2 crore

Exemption: Taxpayers with aggregate turnover up to Rs 2 crore in FY 2026-27 are exempt from filing GSTR-9. This exemption has been in place since FY 2021-22 and has been consistently renewed.

GSTR-9C: Reconciliation Statement

GSTR-9C is a reconciliation statement that reconciles the data in GSTR-9 with the audited annual financial statements. It is required for taxpayers with aggregate turnover exceeding Rs 5 crore.

Financial YearGSTR-9C Due DateWho Must File
FY 2025-26December 31, 2026Taxpayers with turnover > Rs 5 crore
FY 2026-27December 31, 2027Taxpayers with turnover > Rs 5 crore

GSTR-9C is now self-certified (no CA certification required since FY 2020-21). Taxpayers can prepare and sign the reconciliation statement themselves.

GSTR-10: Final Return

GSTR-10 is a one-time return filed by persons whose GST registration has been cancelled or surrendered. It must be filed within 3 months of the date of cancellation or the date of the cancellation order, whichever is later. This return details the stock of inputs, semi-finished goods, finished goods, and capital goods held on the date of cancellation.

Interest on Late Payment of GST

In addition to late fees, interest is charged on the unpaid tax amount from the day after the due date until the date of actual payment.

SituationInterest RateCalculated On
Late payment of tax (Section 50(1))18% per annumNet cash tax liability (total tax minus ITC)
Excess ITC claimed and utilized (Section 50(3))24% per annumAmount of excess ITC utilized
Undue or excess refund (Section 73/74)18% / 24% per annumRefund amount to be recovered

Interest Calculation Example

Suppose your GSTR-3B for April 2026 (due May 20, 2026) is filed on June 19, 2026 — a delay of 30 days. Your net cash tax liability after ITC adjustment is Rs 1,00,000.

  • Interest = Rs 1,00,000 × 18% × 30/365 = Rs 1,479
  • Late fee (assuming turnover up to Rs 1.5 crore) = Rs 50 × 30 = Rs 1,500 (capped at Rs 2,000)
  • Total additional cost = Rs 1,479 + Rs 1,500 = Rs 2,979

Consequences of Persistent Non-Filing

Missing GST return deadlines consistently has cascading consequences beyond just late fees and interest:

Sequential Filing Restriction

The GST portal enforces sequential filing — you cannot file a return for a later period without first filing all pending returns for earlier periods. This means if you miss April's GSTR-3B, you cannot file May's GSTR-3B until April's is filed. Late fees accumulate for all pending periods simultaneously.

E-Way Bill Blocking

If a taxpayer fails to file GSTR-3B for two or more consecutive months (or two consecutive quarters for quarterly filers), their e-way bill generation facility is blocked under Rule 138E. This means you cannot generate e-way bills for transporting goods, effectively halting your logistics operations.

ITC Impact on Buyers

If you fail to file GSTR-1, your B2B customers cannot see your invoices in their GSTR-2B, which means they cannot claim ITC on purchases from you. This can damage business relationships, as customers may refuse to transact with non-compliant suppliers.

Registration Cancellation

The tax officer can cancel your GST registration suo motu if you fail to file returns for a continuous period of 6 months (regular taxpayer) or 3 consecutive quarters (composition taxpayer). Once cancelled, revocation is time-bound and involves filing all pending returns with full late fees and interest.

Assessment and Best Judgment Order

If a registered person fails to file returns for two consecutive tax periods, the officer can initiate best judgment assessment under Section 62, determining the tax liability based on available information. This assessment can result in a significantly higher demand than your actual liability.

Tips to Avoid Missing GST Deadlines

  1. Set calendar reminders 5 days before each due date — This gives you enough buffer to prepare and file. Many deadlines fall close together (GSTR-1 on 11th, GSTR-3B on 20th), so plan your compliance workflow accordingly.
  2. File GSTR-1 before GSTR-3B — Always file GSTR-1 first. This ensures data consistency and allows you to cross-verify the output tax figures before declaring them in GSTR-3B.
  3. Reconcile regularly — Do not wait until the due date to start reconciling your books with the GST portal data. Monthly reconciliation of purchase register with GSTR-2B helps you claim accurate ITC in GSTR-3B.
  4. Use the QRMP scheme if eligible — If your turnover is up to Rs 5 crore, the QRMP scheme reduces your return filing from 24 returns per year (12 GSTR-1 + 12 GSTR-3B) to just 8 (4 GSTR-1 + 4 GSTR-3B), with monthly tax payments via PMT-06.
  5. Maintain digital records — Keep purchase invoices, sales invoices, and bank statements organized digitally. This speeds up return preparation dramatically.
  6. File Nil returns on time — Even if there is no business activity in a period, Nil returns must be filed. The late fee for Nil returns is lower (Rs 20/day, max Rs 500), but it still accumulates and blocks future filings.
  7. Use AI tools for compliance — Tools like DoAide GST Bot can automate data preparation, catch mismatches before filing, and send deadline reminders so you never miss a due date.

Extended Deadlines and Government Relaxations

The government periodically extends GST due dates through notifications, typically in these situations:

  • Natural disasters — States affected by floods, cyclones, or earthquakes often receive deadline extensions.
  • System outages — If the GST portal faces extended downtime near a due date, the CBIC may extend the deadline.
  • Late fee amnesty — The government has announced multiple amnesty schemes waiving late fees for past period returns to encourage compliance.
  • COVID-19 era extensions — During the pandemic, widespread extensions were granted. While this is no longer common, the precedent exists.

Check notifications: Always check the CBIC website (cbic.gov.in) and the GST portal for the latest notifications. Extensions are announced through gazette notifications and are specific to certain return types, taxpayer categories, or regions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the due date for GSTR-3B filing for monthly filers in FY 2026-27?

For regular monthly filers with aggregate turnover above Rs 5 crore, the GSTR-3B due date is the 20th of the month following the tax period. For example, the GSTR-3B for April 2026 is due on May 20, 2026. This due date is fixed throughout the financial year.

What is the late fee for delayed GSTR-1 filing?

The late fee for GSTR-1 is Rs 50 per day of delay (Rs 25 CGST + Rs 25 SGST) for returns with outward supply details, and Rs 20 per day (Rs 10 CGST + Rs 10 SGST) for Nil GSTR-1 returns. The maximum late fee is capped based on turnover: Rs 2,000 for turnover up to Rs 1.5 crore, Rs 5,000 for Rs 1.5-5 crore, and Rs 10,000 for above Rs 5 crore.

What are the GSTR-3B due dates for quarterly filers under the QRMP scheme?

Under the QRMP scheme, GSTR-3B is due on the 22nd of the month following the quarter for Category 1 states (Maharashtra, Gujarat, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, etc.) and on the 24th for Category 2 states (Delhi, UP, Punjab, Rajasthan, etc.). For Q1 (Apr-Jun 2026), the due date is July 22 or July 24, 2026.

When is GSTR-9 annual return due for FY 2025-26?

The GSTR-9 annual return for FY 2025-26 is due on December 31, 2026. Taxpayers with turnover up to Rs 2 crore are exempt from filing GSTR-9. GSTR-9C (reconciliation statement) for taxpayers with turnover above Rs 5 crore is also due on December 31, 2026.

What happens if I miss the GST return filing due date?

Missing the due date triggers late fees (Rs 20-50 per day), interest at 18% per annum on unpaid tax, blocking of subsequent return filing, blocking of e-way bill generation after two consecutive missed periods, and potential suo motu cancellation of registration for persistent non-filing.

What is the interest rate on late payment of GST?

The interest rate on late payment of GST is 18% per annum under Section 50(1), calculated on the net cash tax liability from the day after the due date until payment. For excess ITC claimed and utilized, the interest rate is 24% per annum. Interest is calculated on the net cash liability, not the gross tax amount.

Are GST return due dates extended if they fall on a Sunday or public holiday?

GST return due dates are generally not automatically extended for weekends or holidays. The government may issue specific notifications for extensions in exceptional circumstances, but taxpayers should plan to file before the due date to avoid the risk. Always check the CBIC website for the latest notifications.

What is the PMT-06 challan and when should I pay it under QRMP?

PMT-06 is a challan for monthly tax payments by QRMP scheme taxpayers in the first two months of each quarter. It is due by the 25th of the following month. Taxpayers can use the fixed sum method (35% of preceding quarter's cash liability) or self-assessment. No PMT-06 is needed for the third month — tax is paid through GSTR-3B.

When is the composition scheme return (CMP-08) due?

CMP-08 is a quarterly statement-cum-challan due on the 18th of the month following the quarter. For Q1 (Apr-Jun 2026), it is due on July 18, 2026. The annual return GSTR-4 for composition taxpayers is due by April 30 of the following year.

Can I file GSTR-1 after the due date without penalty?

No, filing GSTR-1 after the due date automatically attracts a late fee of Rs 50 per day (or Rs 20 for Nil returns). The government periodically announces amnesty schemes to waive or reduce late fees. Additionally, GSTR-1 cannot be filed if the preceding period's GSTR-1 or GSTR-3B is pending.
