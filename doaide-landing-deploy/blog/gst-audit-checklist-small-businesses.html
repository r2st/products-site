GST Audit Checklist for Small Businesses in India (2026-27): Everything You Need to Know

A practical, step-by-step guide to preparing for your GST annual return and reconciliation statement, with a complete checklist to keep your small business fully compliant.

What Is a GST Audit and Why Does It Matter?

If you run a small business in India, the phrase "GST audit" can feel intimidating. Visions of mountains of paperwork, last-minute scrambles with your accountant, and the looming threat of penalties tend to surface. But a GST audit, at its core, is simply a systematic review of your business's GST records, returns, and financial statements to ensure everything aligns correctly.

For financial year 2026-27, getting your GST audit checklist in order early is not just smart practice — it is a business necessity. The GST ecosystem has matured significantly since its introduction in 2017. With improved data-matching capabilities on the GST portal, auto-populated return data, and tighter enforcement of Input Tax Credit (ITC) rules, discrepancies between your books and your filed returns are flagged faster than ever.

For small businesses, the stakes are particularly high. Even minor mismatches — a missed invoice here, an incorrect HSN code there — can cascade into ITC reversals, interest charges, or audit notices. This guide walks you through exactly what you need to prepare, organized into a practical checklist you can start working through today.

Key Change

From FY 2020-21 onwards, the mandatory GST audit by a Chartered Accountant or Cost Accountant under Section 35(5) and Section 44(2) of the CGST Act was abolished. Businesses with aggregate turnover exceeding ₹5 crore must now self-certify GSTR-9C (the reconciliation statement) instead of obtaining a third-party audit certificate.

Who Needs to File GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C?

Understanding your filing obligations is the first step. Not every business faces the same requirements when it comes to annual returns and reconciliation.

GSTR-9: Annual Return

Every registered GST taxpayer must file GSTR-9 as their annual return. However, the following categories are exempt:

  • Composition scheme dealers (they file GSTR-9A instead)
  • Casual taxable persons
  • Input Service Distributors (ISDs)
  • Non-resident taxable persons
  • Persons paying TDS under Section 51
  • Persons paying TCS under Section 52 (e-commerce operators)

For recent financial years, the government has exempted taxpayers with aggregate turnover up to ₹2 crore from filing GSTR-9. Keep an eye on notifications for FY 2026-27, as this threshold may be revised.

GSTR-9C: Reconciliation Statement

GSTR-9C is mandatory for businesses with an aggregate annual turnover exceeding ₹5 crore. This is a self-certified reconciliation statement that reconciles the values declared in GSTR-9 with the audited annual financial statements. Even if your turnover falls below this threshold, preparing a GSTR-9C-style reconciliation internally is strongly recommended as it helps catch discrepancies before they become problems.

Turnover GSTR-9 GSTR-9C
Up to ₹2 crore Exempt (subject to notification) Not required
₹2 crore to ₹5 crore Mandatory Not required
Above ₹5 crore Mandatory Mandatory (self-certified)

GSTR-9: What Data to Prepare

GSTR-9 consolidates an entire financial year's worth of GST data. It is split into six parts and covers outward supplies, inward supplies, ITC details, tax paid, and transactions from the previous year reported in the current year. Here is the data you need to gather:

  • Part II — Outward Supplies: All B2B, B2C, exports, SEZ supplies, deemed exports, and advances received and adjusted, categorized by tax rate.
  • Part III — ITC Details: ITC availed (from imports, inward supplies under reverse charge, ISD credits, and all other ITC), ITC reversed, ineligible ITC, and other ITC-related details.
  • Part IV — Tax Paid: Breakdown of tax paid through cash and ITC utilization for IGST, CGST, SGST, and cess.
  • Part V — Transactions of Previous FY: Amendments and additions to outward and inward supplies from the previous year reported in the current year's returns (April to September of the next year).
  • Part VI — Other Information: Demands and refunds, HSN-wise summary of outward and inward supplies, and late fee details.
Important

GSTR-9 cannot be revised once filed. Every figure you enter is final. This makes pre-filing reconciliation absolutely critical — treat this return as a one-shot submission and verify every number before you click "File."

GSTR-9C: The Reconciliation Statement

If your aggregate turnover exceeds ₹5 crore, GSTR-9C requires you to reconcile the values in your audited financial statements with the values declared in GSTR-9. This reconciliation covers:

  • Turnover reconciliation: Gross turnover as per books vs. turnover declared in the annual return, explaining every adjustment (unbilled revenue, advances, unadjusted advances, deemed supply values, credit notes, trade discounts, etc.).
  • Tax paid reconciliation: Tax payable as per the reconciliation vs. tax actually paid as per the return.
  • ITC reconciliation: ITC availed as per books vs. ITC claimed in the annual return, with adjustments for ITC booked in the next year, ITC on goods in transit, and provisional ITC reversals.
  • Expense-wise reconciliation: ITC categorized by major expense heads compared with returns.

Since FY 2020-21, this statement is self-certified by the taxpayer. While a CA audit is no longer mandatory, many businesses still engage their auditor to review the reconciliation for accuracy and completeness, especially given the potential consequences of errors.

Complete GST Audit Checklist for FY 2026-27

Here is your comprehensive, category-wise GST audit checklist. Work through each section systematically. Start 2 to 3 months before your filing deadline to allow time for corrections.

1 Registration and Compliance Documents
  • Verify GST registration certificate and ensure all details (trade name, address, business activities) are current
  • Confirm all amendments to registration (new branches, additional places of business) have been updated on the portal
  • Collect copies of all monthly/quarterly returns filed during the year (GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-2B)
  • Verify that all returns have been filed on time; note any late filings and corresponding late fees paid
  • Confirm active status of GSTIN on the portal — ensure no suo motu cancellation proceedings are pending
2 Sales / Outward Supply Reconciliation
  • Reconcile total sales as per books with total outward supplies declared in GSTR-1 (all periods)
  • Verify B2B invoices in GSTR-1 match your sales register (invoice number, date, value, GSTIN of recipient)
  • Reconcile B2C sales (both large and small) with GSTR-1 declarations
  • Cross-verify export invoices with shipping bills and GSTR-1 export data; confirm IGST refund claims
  • Check for any unbilled revenue at year end and account for advances received with GST implications
  • Verify credit notes and debit notes are correctly reflected in GSTR-1 and within the time limits
  • Reconcile e-commerce operator TCS (GSTR-8) with your GSTR-3B, if applicable
3 Purchase / Inward Supply Reconciliation
  • Match purchase register with GSTR-2B auto-populated data for each return period
  • Identify invoices present in your books but missing from GSTR-2B (supplier may not have filed GSTR-1)
  • Identify invoices present in GSTR-2B but not recorded in your books (potential missed entries)
  • Verify import invoices (Bills of Entry) are correctly reflected and IGST on imports is accounted for
  • Check that all vendor GSTINs are active and valid on the portal
  • Confirm that purchases from composition dealers are not carrying any ITC claims
4 Input Tax Credit (ITC) Verification
  • Verify ITC claimed does not exceed the amount available in GSTR-2B (Rule 36(4) compliance)
  • Check the 180-day payment rule: reverse ITC where payment to the supplier has not been made within 180 days from the date of invoice
  • Review blocked credits under Section 17(5) — ensure no ITC claimed on motor vehicles (unless qualifying), food and beverages, health insurance, club memberships, personal consumption, etc.
  • Verify proportional ITC reversal for goods/services used partly for exempt supplies and partly for taxable supplies (Rule 42 and Rule 43)
  • Check ITC reversal on capital goods used for exempt supplies
  • Verify ITC on goods received in lots/installments is claimed only upon receipt of the last lot
  • Cross-check ITC ledger balance on the GST portal with your books
  • Verify GSTR-2B reconciliation for each month to ensure ITC claimed in GSTR-3B is supported
5 Tax Payment Reconciliation
  • Reconcile tax payable as per books with tax declared in GSTR-3B for each return period
  • Verify correct utilization of ITC across IGST, CGST, and SGST as per the prescribed order
  • Check that interest on late payments (Section 50) has been correctly computed and paid
  • Reconcile electronic cash ledger, credit ledger, and liability ledger balances with your records
  • Confirm that all challans are properly accounted for and reconcile with bank statements
6 HSN/SAC Code Verification
  • Verify correct HSN codes (for goods) and SAC codes (for services) are used on all invoices
  • Cross-check HSN codes with the applicable GST rate to ensure correct tax has been charged
  • Prepare HSN-wise summary for GSTR-9 Part VI (mandatory for businesses with turnover above ₹5 crore)
  • Verify HSN codes used in GSTR-1 match those on your actual invoices and in your books
7 E-Way Bill Compliance
  • Verify e-way bills have been generated for all consignments exceeding ₹50,000
  • Reconcile e-way bill data with GSTR-1 and sales register — flag invoices with e-way bills but no GSTR-1 entry, and vice versa
  • Check validity periods of e-way bills for goods in transit at year end
  • Ensure Part B (vehicle/transport details) is updated where required
8 Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) Verification
  • Identify all supplies subject to reverse charge (Section 9(3) and 9(4) of CGST Act)
  • Verify GST under RCM has been correctly computed and paid via GSTR-3B
  • Confirm that invoices for RCM supplies have been self-generated as required
  • Verify ITC on RCM payments has been claimed correctly and is reflected in GSTR-2B
  • Common RCM items to check: legal services, GTA services, security services, import of services, rent from unregistered persons
9 Credit Note / Debit Note Reconciliation
  • Verify all credit notes are issued within the time limit prescribed under Section 34
  • Reconcile credit notes and debit notes in books with GSTR-1 declarations
  • Ensure corresponding ITC adjustments have been made by recipients for credit notes issued
  • Check that financial credit notes (without GST implications) are not incorrectly reducing tax liability

Common Audit Findings and How to Avoid Them

Year after year, certain errors show up repeatedly during GST audits and annual return preparation. Being aware of these common pitfalls can save your business significant time, money, and stress.

1. GSTR-1 vs. GSTR-3B Mismatch

The most frequent finding is a mismatch between the taxable value and tax amounts declared in GSTR-1 (sales details) and GSTR-3B (summary return with tax payment). This typically happens when amendments or credit notes are reflected in one return but not the other. Run a monthly comparison to catch discrepancies early.

2. ITC Claimed Beyond GSTR-2B

With Rule 36(4) in effect, ITC claimed in GSTR-3B cannot exceed the amount available in GSTR-2B (plus a small tolerance, if any, as notified). Businesses that claim ITC based on purchase invoices without verifying GSTR-2B risk excess claims and subsequent reversals with interest.

3. Incorrect HSN Classification

Applying the wrong HSN or SAC code can result in incorrect tax rates. This is especially common for businesses dealing in goods that straddle multiple classifications. When in doubt, refer to the HSN explanatory notes or use a reliable classification tool.

4. Missing Reverse Charge Payments

Many small businesses overlook RCM obligations entirely, particularly for services like legal fees, GTA (goods transport agency) services, or rent paid to unregistered landlords. Set up your accounting system to flag RCM-liable transactions automatically.

5. Late ITC Reversal Under the 180-Day Rule

If you have not paid your supplier within 180 days of the invoice date, the ITC must be reversed. Businesses that track payables on a cash basis often miss this. Implement an aging schedule that automatically flags invoices approaching the 180-day mark.

6. Ignoring Credit Notes Time Limit

Credit notes reducing tax liability must be issued before 30th November of the following financial year (or the date of filing the annual return, whichever is earlier). Missing this deadline means you cannot reduce your tax liability for that period.

When to Start Preparing: A Practical Timeline

The standard deadline for GSTR-9 filing is 31st December of the year following the financial year. For FY 2026-27, that means 31st December 2027 (subject to extension). Here is a suggested preparation timeline:

September 2027 — Month 1

Begin gathering all filed returns (GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, GSTR-2B) for the full financial year. Start the sales reconciliation — compare GSTR-1 with your books of accounts. Flag all discrepancies.

October 2027 — Month 2

Complete purchase and ITC reconciliation. Verify GSTR-2B data against your purchase register. Check for 180-day payment defaults, blocked credit claims, and Rule 36(4) compliance. Reconcile RCM entries.

November 2027 — Month 3

Finalize all reconciliations. Prepare HSN-wise summary. Draft GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C (if applicable). Issue any pending credit notes before 30th November. Review the draft with your CA or tax advisor.

December 2027 — Filing

Perform a final review of GSTR-9 and GSTR-9C. File before 31st December. Remember — GSTR-9 cannot be revised once filed, so this final check is critical.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who needs to file GSTR-9?

Every registered taxpayer under GST must file GSTR-9 as an annual return, except composition scheme dealers (who file GSTR-9A), casual taxable persons, input service distributors, non-resident taxable persons, and persons paying TDS under Section 51. Taxpayers with aggregate turnover up to ₹2 crore have been exempt in recent years, subject to government notification each year.

Is GST audit by a CA still mandatory?

No. From FY 2020-21 onwards, the mandatory GST audit by a Chartered Accountant or Cost Accountant under Section 35(5) and Section 44(2) was abolished. Taxpayers with turnover exceeding ₹5 crore must now self-certify GSTR-9C (the reconciliation statement). However, many businesses still engage a CA for review as a best practice.

What is the turnover threshold for filing GSTR-9C?

Businesses with an aggregate annual turnover exceeding ₹5 crore in a financial year are required to file GSTR-9C along with GSTR-9. The GSTR-9C is a self-certified reconciliation statement that reconciles the values in your audited financial statements with the values in your annual return.

What is the deadline for filing GSTR-9 for FY 2026-27?

The standard deadline for filing GSTR-9 is 31st December of the following financial year. For FY 2026-27, the deadline would be 31st December 2027, unless extended by the government through a notification. It is advisable to start preparation at least 2–3 months in advance.

Can I revise GSTR-9 after filing?

No, GSTR-9 cannot be revised once filed. This makes it absolutely critical to verify all data thoroughly before submission. Any errors will need to be corrected in the next year's returns, where applicable. Always perform a complete reconciliation and review before clicking "File."

What happens if I do not file GSTR-9?

Non-filing of GSTR-9 attracts a late fee of ₹200 per day (₹100 CGST + ₹100 SGST), subject to a maximum of 0.5% of turnover in the state or union territory. Additionally, non-filing can lead to scrutiny notices and may affect your ability to generate e-way bills or file other returns.

What is Rule 36(4) and how does it affect my ITC claims?

Rule 36(4) restricts the amount of ITC a recipient can claim. ITC on invoices or debit notes that are not reflected in GSTR-2B cannot be claimed. This means your ITC in GSTR-3B must not exceed the ITC available in your auto-populated GSTR-2B. You must verify this reconciliation for every return period to avoid excess ITC claims.
