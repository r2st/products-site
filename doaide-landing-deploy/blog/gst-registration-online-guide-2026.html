GST Registration Online: Complete Step-by-Step Guide 2026

Published on October 6, 2026 • 15 min read • Updated for FY 2026-27

GST registration is the foundational step for every business operating in India under the Goods and Services Tax regime. Whether you are a startup founder launching your first venture, a freelancer crossing the turnover threshold, or an established business expanding to a new state, obtaining a GSTIN (Goods and Services Tax Identification Number) is mandatory before you can collect tax, claim input tax credit, or sell on most e-commerce platforms.

This comprehensive guide walks you through the entire GST registration process in 2026 — from determining your eligibility and gathering documents to completing the online application on the GST portal and tracking your approval. We also cover common mistakes that delay approval, the different types of GST registration, and what to do after you receive your GSTIN.

The entire process is free and fully online on the official GST portal at gst.gov.in. There is no government fee for GST registration — beware of any intermediary charging you for registration itself.

Who Needs GST Registration?

Not every business in India requires GST registration. The requirement depends on your aggregate turnover, the nature of your business activity, and whether any mandatory registration conditions apply to you under Section 22 and Section 24 of the CGST Act, 2017.

Turnover-Based Threshold Limits

The GST registration threshold varies based on whether you supply goods or services, and the state in which your principal place of business is located.

CategoryRegular StatesSpecial Category States*
Suppliers of goods onlyRs 40 lakhRs 20 lakh
Suppliers of services onlyRs 20 lakhRs 10 lakh
Mixed suppliers (goods + services)Rs 20 lakhRs 10 lakh

Special Category States: Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura, Meghalaya, Arunachal Pradesh, Sikkim, Uttarakhand, and Himachal Pradesh have lower threshold limits. Jammu & Kashmir and Assam have opted for the regular threshold of Rs 40 lakh for goods and Rs 20 lakh for services.

Mandatory Registration (Irrespective of Turnover)

Under Section 24 of the CGST Act, certain categories of persons must register under GST regardless of their turnover. These include:

  1. Inter-state suppliers — Any person making taxable supplies of goods or services from one state to another must register, with no turnover exemption (subject to certain notification-based exceptions for goods).
  2. Casual taxable persons — Persons who occasionally undertake transactions involving supply of goods or services in a state where they have no fixed place of business.
  3. Non-resident taxable persons — Persons who occasionally supply goods or services but have no fixed establishment in India.
  4. Persons liable to pay tax under reverse charge — Recipients of supplies where the tax liability falls on the recipient instead of the supplier under Section 9(3) or 9(4).
  5. E-commerce operators — Every electronic commerce operator required to collect tax at source (TCS) under Section 52.
  6. Persons supplying through e-commerce operators — Persons who supply goods or services through e-commerce platforms are generally required to register, though recent notifications exempt intra-state suppliers of goods below the threshold.
  7. Input Service Distributors (ISD) — Entities that receive invoices for input services and distribute the credit to their branches or units.
  8. Persons liable to deduct TDS — Government departments and specified entities required to deduct tax at source under Section 51.
  9. Agents of a supplier — Persons who make taxable supplies on behalf of other taxable persons as their agents.
  10. Online information and database access or retrieval (OIDAR) services providers — Non-resident providers of digital services to unregistered persons in India.

Voluntary Registration

Even if your turnover is below the threshold limit, you may choose to register voluntarily under Section 25(3) of the CGST Act. Voluntary registration offers several benefits:

  • Input tax credit — You can claim credit on GST paid on your business purchases and expenses, reducing your effective cost.
  • E-commerce eligibility — Most platforms like Amazon, Flipkart, and Myntra require a valid GSTIN to list products.
  • Business credibility — A GSTIN adds legitimacy and builds trust with B2B customers who need your invoice for their ITC claims.
  • Inter-state supply — You can sell across state borders without worrying about violating registration requirements.
  • Government tenders — Many government contracts require vendors to have GST registration.

Important: Once you register voluntarily, you are bound by all GST compliance obligations — filing returns, maintaining books of account, issuing tax invoices, and paying tax. You cannot simply let the registration lapse; you must formally cancel it if you wish to deregister.

Regular vs Composition: Which Should You Choose?

The most common choice new businesses face is between Regular and Composition Scheme registration. Here is a quick decision framework:

  • Choose Regular if you make inter-state sales, sell on e-commerce platforms, need to claim input tax credit, supply to B2B customers who need your invoice for their ITC, or deal in exempt and taxable supplies simultaneously.
  • Choose Composition if you sell primarily within your state, deal with end consumers (B2C), want simplified compliance with quarterly filing, and your turnover is within limits. Note that composition dealers cannot issue tax invoices and their customers cannot claim ITC on purchases from them.

GST Registration Process: Step-by-Step Walkthrough

The GST registration application is filed online through Form GST REG-01 on the GST portal. The process involves two parts: Part A (basic verification) and Part B (detailed application). Here is the complete walkthrough.

Step 1: Generate a Temporary Reference Number (TRN)

  1. Visit the GST portal at gst.gov.in
  2. Click on Services > Registration > New Registration
  3. Select "New Registration" radio button
  4. Choose your category: Taxpayer, GST Practitioner, or TDS/TCS
  5. Select your State and District
  6. Enter the Legal Name of Business (as on PAN)
  7. Enter the PAN of the business or proprietor
  8. Enter your email address and mobile number
  9. Click Proceed — you will receive OTPs on both your email and mobile
  10. Enter both OTPs and click Proceed
  11. A Temporary Reference Number (TRN) will be generated and sent to your email and mobile. Note this down — you will need it to resume your application.

Step 2: Log In with TRN and Complete Part B

  1. Go to Services > Registration > New Registration
  2. Select the "Temporary Reference Number (TRN)" radio button
  3. Enter your TRN and the CAPTCHA
  4. Click Proceed — OTPs will be sent to your registered email and mobile
  5. Enter the OTPs to log in to your saved application

Time Limit: You must complete and submit Part B of the application within 15 days of generating the TRN. If the TRN expires, you will need to generate a new one by repeating Part A.

Step 3: Fill Business Details Tab

  • Trade name — The name under which your business operates (can be different from the legal name)
  • Constitution of business — Select from Proprietorship, Partnership, LLP, Private Limited Company, Public Limited Company, HUF, Society, Trust, etc.
  • District, Sector, Ward, Centre, State Jurisdiction, Commissionerate — These auto-populate based on your address. Verify them carefully.
  • Date of commencement of business — The date you started or intend to start business operations
  • Date on which liability to register arises — If registering because you crossed the threshold, enter that date. For voluntary registration, this will be the application date.
  • Reason for registration — Select from dropdown: crossing threshold, inter-state supply, voluntary, casual taxable person, etc.

Step 4: Fill Promoter/Partners Tab

Enter details of all promoters, partners, directors, or the proprietor as applicable:

  • Full name (as on PAN), date of birth, mobile number, email
  • PAN number, Aadhaar number
  • Designation (Proprietor, Partner, Director, Managing Director, Karta, etc.)
  • Residential address
  • Upload photograph (JPEG, max 100 KB)
  • Mark the primary authorized signatory who will sign returns and other documents

Step 5: Fill Authorized Signatory Tab

Select the person who will be the authorized signatory for GST compliance. This person will:

  • Sign and file GST returns
  • Respond to notices
  • File applications for amendments, cancellation, and refunds
  • Appear before the tax authorities on behalf of the business

You can choose an existing promoter/partner/director or add a separate authorized representative. Upload a letter of authorization or board resolution as applicable.

Step 6: Fill Principal Place of Business Tab

  • Enter the complete address with PIN code, state, and district
  • Provide contact details (phone, email) for the business premises
  • Select the nature of possession: Own, Rented, Leased, Shared, or Consent
  • Upload proof of address as per the nature of possession
  • Select the nature of business activities carried out at this location: Manufacturing, Trading, Warehouse, Services, Office, etc.

Step 7: Add Additional Place of Business (If Any)

If you have multiple offices, warehouses, branches, godowns, or manufacturing units in the same state, add each as an additional place of business. For each additional location, provide the address, nature of possession, and proof of address.

Note: Each state requires a separate GST registration. If you have business premises in multiple states, you need to file separate applications for each state. The additional place of business tab is only for locations within the same state as your principal place of business.

Step 8: Fill Goods and Services Tab

Declare the top goods and services you supply by entering the relevant HSN codes (for goods) and SAC codes (for services). You must enter at least one code. Choose the codes that best represent your primary business activities. You can add up to 5 goods and 5 services during registration (additional items can be added later through amendment).

Step 9: Fill Bank Accounts Tab

Enter details of your business bank accounts. While this section can be skipped during registration and added within 45 days of registration, it is recommended to provide it upfront to avoid delays. You need:

  • Bank account number
  • Account type (Savings, Current, Cash Credit, etc.)
  • IFSC code
  • Bank name and branch name (auto-populated from IFSC)
  • Upload cancelled cheque or bank statement

Step 10: Choose Aadhaar Authentication

You will be asked whether you want to opt for Aadhaar authentication. This is a critical decision that affects your processing time:

AspectWith Aadhaar AuthenticationWithout Aadhaar Authentication
Processing time3 working days (deemed approval if no action by officer)Up to 30 days (physical verification may be initiated)
Physical verificationNot required (unless high-risk flags)May be initiated for the principal place of business
RequirementAadhaar of all promoters/partners/directors must be linked to mobile for OTPNo Aadhaar authentication needed
Biometric optionAvailable at GST Suvidha Kendra if OTP failsN/A

Step 11: Verify and Submit with DSC or EVC

After completing all tabs, preview your application to verify all details are correct. Then submit using one of the following methods:

  • DSC (Digital Signature Certificate) — Mandatory for companies and LLPs. Use a valid Class 2 or Class 3 DSC of the authorized signatory.
  • EVC (Electronic Verification Code) — Available for proprietorship, partnership, HUF, and other non-corporate entities. An OTP is sent to the Aadhaar-linked mobile number of the authorized signatory.
  • E-Sign — Aadhaar-based electronic signature using OTP verification.

Upon successful submission, you will receive an Application Reference Number (ARN) via SMS and email. This ARN is your application's tracking number.

If you opted for Aadhaar authentication and the officer does not take any action within 3 working days of Aadhaar authentication, your registration is deemed to be approved. The system automatically generates the GSTIN and registration certificate.

Tracking Your Application

To check the status of your application:

  1. Visit gst.gov.in
  2. Go to Services > Registration > Track Application Status
  3. Enter your ARN
  4. The status will show one of the following:
    • Pending for Processing — Application is in the officer's queue
    • Pending for Clarification — Officer has raised a query; check your email and GST portal for the notice
    • Site Verification Initiated — Physical verification has been ordered
    • Approved — Your GSTIN has been issued
    • Rejected — Your application has been rejected with reasons

Understanding Your GSTIN

Once approved, you receive a 15-digit GSTIN. The structure of GSTIN encodes important information:

PositionDigitsMeaning
1-2State codeTwo-digit code representing the state (e.g., 27 for Maharashtra, 07 for Delhi, 29 for Karnataka)
3-12PANYour 10-character PAN (Permanent Account Number)
13Entity numberAssigned based on the number of registrations within a state under the same PAN (1-9, then A-Z)
14DefaultCurrently "Z" by default
15Check digitA checksum character for error detection

For example, if your PAN is ABCDE1234F and you are registered in Maharashtra (state code 27), your GSTIN might be 27ABCDE1234F1Z5.

What to Do After Getting Your GSTIN

Getting your GSTIN is just the beginning. Here are the critical steps you must take immediately after registration:

  1. Download your Registration Certificate — Log in to the GST portal and download Form GST REG-06 from the registration section. This is your official GST registration certificate.
  2. Display your GSTIN — Section 46 of the CGST Act requires you to display your GSTIN prominently at your principal place of business and at every additional place of business.
  3. Update bank account details — If you skipped this during registration, add your bank account details within 45 days.
  4. Issue proper tax invoices — Start issuing GST-compliant tax invoices with your GSTIN, HSN/SAC codes, and applicable tax rates.
  5. Set up your filing calendar — Understand your return filing obligations (GSTR-1, GSTR-3B) and their due dates. Missing the first return attracts penalties.
  6. Opt for the composition scheme — If eligible and desired, opt for the composition scheme within 30 days of registration using Form GST CMP-02.
  7. Opt for the QRMP scheme — If your turnover is up to Rs 5 crore and you prefer quarterly filing, opt for the QRMP scheme from the next quarter onwards.
  8. Maintain books of account — Under Section 35, every registered person must maintain proper records of production, inward and outward supply, stock, ITC availed, and output tax payable.

GST Registration for E-Commerce Sellers

E-commerce has specific registration requirements that differ from traditional businesses. If you plan to sell on platforms like Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho, or your own website, here is what you need to know.

When Registration Is Mandatory

Under Section 24 of the CGST Act, registration is mandatory for:

  • E-commerce operators — Platforms like Amazon, Flipkart, Swiggy, Zomato that facilitate supplies and are required to collect TCS.
  • Suppliers through e-commerce — Generally, persons who supply through e-commerce platforms must register irrespective of turnover.

Exemption for Small Sellers

Notification No. 34/2023 (as amended) exempts suppliers of goods through e-commerce platforms from compulsory registration if:

  • Their aggregate turnover does not exceed the applicable threshold limit
  • They make only intra-state supplies (within the same state)
  • They have a PAN and register under the special provision
  • They do not make inter-state supplies

Practical Reality: Most e-commerce platforms still require a valid GSTIN as part of their seller onboarding process, regardless of whether the notification exempts you. It is generally advisable to register for GST before starting e-commerce operations.

Amendment of GST Registration

After registration, you may need to update your registration details. Changes are classified as core fields (requiring officer approval) and non-core fields (self-approved).

Core Fields (Approval Required)

Changes to the following fields require submission of Form GST REG-14 and officer approval:

  • Legal name of the business
  • Principal place of business (change of address within the same state)
  • Addition or deletion of additional place of business
  • Change in partners, directors, or karta (addition, removal, or change in details)

Non-Core Fields (Self-Approved)

Changes to the following fields take effect immediately without officer approval:

  • Trade name
  • Bank account details
  • Authorized signatory
  • Mobile number and email address
  • Goods and services (HSN/SAC codes)

Important: You cannot change your state through an amendment. If your business moves to a different state, you must cancel your existing registration and apply for a new one in the new state. Similarly, PAN changes require a new registration.

Cancellation of GST Registration

You may cancel your GST registration voluntarily or it may be cancelled by the tax officer under certain conditions.

Voluntary Cancellation (By Taxpayer)

You can apply for cancellation using Form GST REG-16 if:

  • Your business is discontinued, transferred, amalgamated, or demerged
  • There is a change in the constitution of business (e.g., proprietorship converting to a company)
  • Your turnover falls below the threshold limit (for voluntarily registered persons)
  • The registered person (other than the person who has voluntarily registered) is no longer liable to be registered

When filing for cancellation, you must:

  1. File all pending returns up to the date of cancellation
  2. Pay any outstanding tax liability including interest
  3. Declare the stock of inputs, semi-finished goods, finished goods, and capital goods held on the date of cancellation
  4. Pay an amount equal to the credit on such stock or the tax on the transaction value of the stock, whichever is higher
  5. Specify the date from which cancellation is sought

Cancellation by Tax Officer (Suo Motu)

The tax officer may cancel your registration by issuing Form GST REG-17 in the following situations:

  • Non-filing of returns for a continuous period of 6 months (regular taxpayer)
  • Non-filing of returns for 3 consecutive quarters (composition taxpayer)
  • Registration obtained by means of fraud, wilful misstatement, or suppression of facts
  • Violation of anti-profiteering provisions
  • Non-commencement of business within 6 months of voluntary registration

Revocation of Cancelled Registration

If your registration has been cancelled by the tax officer (suo motu), you can apply for revocation of cancellation using Form GST REG-21 within 90 days of the cancellation order. The process involves:

  1. Filing all pending returns for the period up to the date of cancellation
  2. Paying all outstanding tax, interest, late fees, and penalties
  3. Applying for revocation within 90 days (extendable up to 180 days by Additional/Joint Commissioner)
  4. The officer will either approve the revocation (REG-22) or reject it (REG-05) within 30 days

Amnesty Schemes: The government periodically announces amnesty schemes that allow taxpayers whose registration was cancelled for non-filing to revoke the cancellation beyond the normal 90-day window by paying all pending dues. Check the CBIC notifications for any ongoing amnesty programs.

Common Mistakes to Avoid During GST Registration

Based on the most frequent reasons for application rejection and delay, here are the mistakes you should avoid:

1. Mismatch Between PAN and Business Name

The legal name of the business in the application must exactly match the name on the PAN card. For proprietorships, this is the proprietor's name as on their personal PAN. For companies, it is the company name as registered with MCA. Even minor discrepancies like "Pvt" vs "Private" can trigger a clarification notice.

2. Incorrect Address Proof

The address on your proof document must match the address entered in the application. If operating from rented premises, a rent agreement alone is not sufficient — you also need a NOC from the landlord and a utility bill for the premises. The electricity bill should be recent (within the last 2 months) and in the name of the owner or the business.

3. Uploading Unclear or Incorrect Documents

Scanned documents must be legible and complete. Common issues include blurry scans, cropped documents where important information is cut off, password-protected PDFs, and files that exceed the size limit. Ensure every page of multi-page documents like partnership deeds is included.

4. Not Linking Aadhaar to Mobile

If you opt for Aadhaar authentication, your Aadhaar must be linked to an active mobile number. If it is not linked, the OTP verification will fail. You can check your Aadhaar-mobile link status on the UIDAI website and update it at an Aadhaar Seva Kendra if needed.

5. Wrong Selection of State and Jurisdiction

The state selected must match the state of your principal place of business. The jurisdiction (ward, sector, range, and commissionerate) auto-populates based on your address, but verify it carefully. An incorrect jurisdiction can lead to issues with future assessments and audits.

6. Missing Bank Account Details

While bank account details can be added within 45 days of registration, it is best to provide them during registration itself. Some officers raise clarifications when bank details are missing, and the absence of bank account verification can delay the approval process.

7. Not Responding to Clarification Notices on Time

If the officer issues a clarification notice (Form GST REG-03), you have only 7 working days to respond via Form GST REG-04. Failure to respond within this time results in automatic rejection of the application. Monitor your registered email and the GST portal dashboard regularly after filing.

8. Incorrect Constitution of Business

Selecting the wrong business constitution (e.g., choosing "Partnership" when you are an LLP, or "Company" when you are a One Person Company) creates a mismatch with your constitutional documents and will be flagged during review.

9. Applying from the Wrong State for E-Commerce

E-commerce sellers sometimes apply for registration in the state where the e-commerce operator's warehouse is located instead of the state where their own principal place of business is located. Registration is tied to your principal place of business, not the warehouse location.

10. Not Understanding the Difference Between Legal Name and Trade Name

The legal name is the name as per PAN — for a proprietor, it is their personal name; for a company, it is the registered name. The trade name is the brand or business name you operate under. Many applicants enter their trade name in the legal name field, causing PAN verification failure.

Registration for Special Categories

Casual Taxable Person

A casual taxable person is someone who occasionally undertakes transactions involving supply of goods or services in a state where they have no fixed place of business. Common examples include:

  • An artisan from Rajasthan selling handicrafts at a fair in Delhi
  • A caterer from Mumbai providing services at an event in Pune
  • A manufacturer setting up a temporary stall at an exhibition in another state

Key requirements for casual taxable person registration:

  • Apply at least 5 days before commencing business operations in the state
  • Pay advance tax using Form GST REG-27 equivalent to the estimated tax liability for the intended period of operations
  • The registration is valid for 90 days (or the period specified in the application, whichever is earlier)
  • Can be extended for a further period of 90 days by filing Form GST REG-11

Non-Resident Taxable Person

Non-resident taxable persons who occasionally supply goods or services in India but have no fixed establishment in the country must:

  • Apply for registration at least 5 days before commencing business operations
  • Pay advance tax equivalent to the estimated tax liability
  • Appoint an authorized representative in India
  • Provide a valid passport as identification (instead of PAN and Aadhaar)
  • Registration is valid for 90 days, extendable for another 90 days
  • File GSTR-5 monthly instead of GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B

Input Service Distributor (ISD)

An office of a supplier that receives invoices for input services and distributes the credit to its branches or units must register separately as an ISD. The ISD registration is separate from the regular GSTIN — a business with an ISD unit will have two GSTINs: one for its regular operations and one for the ISD.

TDS/TCS Registration

Government departments and establishments required to deduct TDS under Section 51 must register using Form GST REG-07. E-commerce operators required to collect TCS under Section 52 must also register. These registrations use the TAN (Tax Deduction Account Number) instead of PAN.

Migrating to GST from Old Tax Regime

While most businesses have already migrated from the pre-GST era (VAT, Service Tax, Central Excise) to GST since 2017, some situations still arise where migration-related knowledge is relevant:

  • Acquiring a business that held a pre-GST registration — the new owner needs fresh GST registration
  • Reopening a business that was registered under VAT/Service Tax but never migrated to GST — a new GST registration is required
  • Resolving legacy disputes where the transition credit from the old regime is being contested

Recent Changes and Updates (2026)

The GST registration process has seen several improvements and changes in recent years:

  • Aadhaar authentication speedup — Applications with Aadhaar authentication are now processed within 3 working days, with deemed approval if no action is taken.
  • Biometric authentication — High-risk applications identified by the system may require biometric authentication at a GST Suvidha Kendra to prevent fraudulent registrations.
  • PAN-Aadhaar linking requirement — PAN must be linked to Aadhaar for the application to be processed.
  • E-commerce seller exemption — Intra-state suppliers of goods through e-commerce platforms below the turnover threshold are exempted from mandatory registration, subject to conditions.
  • Multiple registrations in a state — The 54th GST Council recommended allowing separate registration for different places of business within a state.
  • Enhanced physical verification — For applications flagged as high-risk, the system now requires physical verification of the business premises with geo-tagged photographs.
  • Real-time PAN validation — The portal now validates PAN details in real-time with the Income Tax database, reducing mismatches.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the turnover limit for GST registration in 2026?

The GST registration threshold is Rs 40 lakh aggregate turnover for goods suppliers and Rs 20 lakh for service providers in most states. For special category states (Manipur, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura, Meghalaya, Arunachal Pradesh, Sikkim, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh), the thresholds are Rs 20 lakh for goods and Rs 10 lakh for services.

How long does GST registration take?

GST registration typically takes 3 to 7 working days from the date of application if all documents are in order and no clarification is sought by the officer. If a clarification notice is issued, you get 7 working days to respond, after which the officer must approve or reject within 7 working days. With Aadhaar authentication, the process can be completed in as little as 3 working days.

Can I register for GST voluntarily even if my turnover is below the threshold?

Yes, any person can apply for voluntary GST registration under Section 25(3) of the CGST Act, regardless of their turnover. Voluntary registration allows you to collect GST from customers, claim input tax credit on purchases, and sell on e-commerce platforms. However, once registered voluntarily, you must comply with all GST filing obligations.

What documents are required for GST registration for a proprietorship firm?

For a proprietorship firm, you need: PAN card of the proprietor, Aadhaar card, passport-size photograph, proof of business address (electricity bill, rent agreement with NOC, or property tax receipt), bank account details (cancelled cheque or bank statement), and a declaration on the nature of business activity.

Is Aadhaar authentication mandatory for GST registration?

Aadhaar authentication is not mandatory but is strongly recommended. If you opt for Aadhaar authentication, your application may be approved within 3 working days without physical verification. Without it, the officer may initiate physical verification, extending the processing time to up to 30 days.

Can I have multiple GST registrations in the same state?

Yes, under Section 25(2) of the CGST Act, a person having multiple business verticals in a state may obtain separate GST registrations for each business vertical. Each registration will receive a separate GSTIN and must maintain separate books of accounts and file separate returns.

What is the penalty for not registering under GST when liable?

If a person who is liable to register under GST fails to do so, they face a penalty of Rs 10,000 or the amount of tax evaded, whichever is higher, under Section 122 of the CGST Act. Additionally, they must pay the tax due along with interest at 18% per annum from the date they were liable to register.

How do I track my GST registration application status?

You can track your application status on the GST portal (gst.gov.in) by going to Services > Registration > Track Application Status and entering your ARN (Application Reference Number). The status will show as Pending for Processing, Pending for Clarification, Approved, or Rejected.

Can I cancel my GST registration?

Yes, you can apply for voluntary cancellation of GST registration by filing Form GST REG-16 on the GST portal if your business is discontinued, transferred, or your turnover falls below the threshold. You must file all pending returns, pay any outstanding tax liability, and report the stock of inputs and capital goods held on the date of cancellation.

Do e-commerce sellers need GST registration regardless of turnover?

Generally yes, as per Section 24 of the CGST Act, persons supplying goods or services through an e-commerce operator must register under GST compulsorily. However, a notification exempts intra-state suppliers of goods through e-commerce platforms from compulsory registration if their aggregate turnover is below the threshold limit, subject to certain conditions.
