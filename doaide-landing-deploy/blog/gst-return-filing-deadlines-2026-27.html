GST Return Filing Deadlines 2026-27: Complete Month-Wise Calendar for Indian Businesses

Missing a GST return filing deadline can cost your business anywhere from a few hundred to several thousand rupees in late fees and interest. This comprehensive guide provides every deadline you need for FY 2026-27, organized month-by-month so you never pay a penalty again.

Why Tracking GST Deadlines Matters

The Goods and Services Tax regime in India operates on a strict calendar of return filings. For the roughly 1.4 crore active GST registrations in India, staying on top of these deadlines is not optional — it is a legal obligation with real financial consequences. Every day of delay attracts late fees, interest charges accumulate on unpaid tax, and persistent non-compliance can even result in the cancellation of your GST registration.

Beyond the direct cost of penalties, missed deadlines create cascading problems. Your buyers cannot claim Input Tax Credit (ITC) until your returns are filed, which strains business relationships. Your own ability to file subsequent returns gets blocked, creating a backlog that becomes increasingly expensive to clear. E-way bill generation can be suspended after two consecutive months of non-filing, effectively halting your ability to transport goods.

For FY 2026-27, the filing schedule follows the same structure established in recent years, with monthly and quarterly options depending on your turnover. This guide covers every return type, every deadline, and every penalty so you can plan your compliance calendar with confidence.

Understanding GST Return Types

Before diving into the calendar, it is important to understand what each return covers and who needs to file it.

GSTR-1: Outward Supply Statement

GSTR-1 captures all outward supplies (sales) made during the period. It includes invoice-level details for B2B transactions and consolidated details for B2C transactions. This return is critical because it feeds into your buyers' GSTR-2B, enabling them to claim ITC. Monthly filers must submit GSTR-1 by the 11th of the following month. Quarterly filers under the QRMP scheme file it by the 13th of the month following the quarter.

GSTR-3B: Summary Return with Tax Payment

GSTR-3B is the summary return where you declare your total output tax liability, claim ITC, and pay the net tax due. This is where the actual tax payment happens. Monthly filers must file GSTR-3B by the 20th of the following month. Quarterly filers get a due date of the 22nd or 24th of the month following the quarter, depending on their state.

GSTR-9: Annual Return

GSTR-9 is the annual consolidation of all your monthly or quarterly returns for the entire financial year. It provides a complete picture of your outward supplies, inward supplies, ITC claimed, and tax paid. Filing GSTR-9 is mandatory for all regular taxpayers with aggregate turnover exceeding Rs. 2 crore. The due date for FY 2026-27 is December 31, 2027.

GSTR-9C: Reconciliation Statement

GSTR-9C is a reconciliation statement between the annual return (GSTR-9) and the audited financial statements. It is required for taxpayers with aggregate turnover exceeding Rs. 5 crore in the financial year. Like GSTR-9, it is due by December 31, 2027 for FY 2026-27.

CMP-08: Composition Scheme Return

Businesses registered under the Composition Scheme file CMP-08 instead of GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B. This is a quarterly statement-cum-challan for payment of tax, due by the 18th of the month following the quarter. Composition dealers also file an annual return in Form GSTR-4, due by April 30 of the following year.

QRMP Scheme

The Quarterly Return Monthly Payment (QRMP) scheme is available to taxpayers with aggregate annual turnover up to Rs. 5 crore. Under QRMP, you file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B quarterly but pay tax monthly using either a fixed-sum method or the self-assessment method through Form GST PMT-06.

GST Late Fees and Penalties for Delayed Filing

The financial consequences of missing GST return filing deadlines are twofold: late fees charged per day of delay, and interest on any unpaid tax liability.

Interest on Late Payment

In addition to late fees, interest at 18% per annum is charged on the outstanding tax liability from the day after the due date until the actual date of payment. If you have claimed excess ITC, interest is charged at 24% per annum on the excess amount.

Other Consequences of Non-Filing

  • Return filing blockage: You cannot file a return for a subsequent period until all prior returns are filed. This creates a cascading backlog.
  • ITC blockage for buyers: Your buyers will not see your invoices in their GSTR-2B until you file your GSTR-1, blocking their ITC claims.
  • E-way bill suspension: If you fail to file returns for two consecutive months (or two consecutive quarters under QRMP), your ability to generate e-way bills is suspended.
  • Registration cancellation: Persistent non-filing for six consecutive months can lead to cancellation of your GST registration by the tax officer.
  • Best judgment assessment: The tax officer can make a best judgment assessment under Section 62 if you fail to file returns despite notices.

7 Tips to Never Miss a GST Filing Deadline

Staying compliant does not have to be stressful. Here are practical strategies that successful businesses use to stay on top of their GST calendar.

  1. Set up automated calendar reminders. Add recurring reminders for the 8th (for GSTR-1) and 17th (for GSTR-3B) of each month — giving yourself a 3-day buffer before the actual deadline.
  2. Use the Invoice Furnishing Facility (IFF). QRMP taxpayers can upload B2B invoices monthly using IFF by the 13th of the following month, ensuring your buyers get timely ITC even though your return is quarterly.
  3. Reconcile your books monthly. Do not wait until the filing date to reconcile sales, purchases, and ITC. Monthly reconciliation catches discrepancies early and makes filing faster.
  4. Maintain a dedicated compliance calendar. Create a shared calendar with your accountant or tax consultant that tracks all GST deadlines, advance tax dates, and TDS due dates in one place.
  5. File nil returns on time. Even if you have no transactions, file your nil return by the deadline. Nil returns still attract late fees if filed late, and non-filing triggers the same blockage consequences.
  6. Keep your GST portal credentials accessible. Ensure your authorized signatory details, DSC, and EVC are up to date. Last-minute login issues on the GST portal are a common cause of delayed filings.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the due date for GSTR-1 filing in 2026-27?

For monthly filers, the GSTR-1 due date is the 11th of the month following the tax period. For example, GSTR-1 for April 2026 is due on May 11, 2026. For quarterly filers under the QRMP scheme, GSTR-1 is due on the 13th of the month following the quarter — so Q1 (April-June 2026) GSTR-1 is due on July 13, 2026.

What is the late fee for missing GST return filing deadlines?

The GST late fee is Rs. 50 per day (Rs. 25 CGST + Rs. 25 SGST) for regular returns and Rs. 20 per day (Rs. 10 CGST + Rs. 10 SGST) for nil returns. The maximum late fee is capped based on your turnover: Rs. 2,000 for turnover up to Rs. 1.5 crore, Rs. 5,000 for turnover between Rs. 1.5 crore and Rs. 5 crore, and Rs. 10,000 for turnover above Rs. 5 crore.

When is GSTR-9 annual return due for FY 2026-27?

The GSTR-9 annual return for FY 2026-27 is due on December 31, 2027. The reconciliation statement GSTR-9C (for businesses with turnover above Rs. 5 crore) is also due on the same date. Composition dealers must file their annual return (GSTR-4) by April 30, 2027.

What is the QRMP scheme and who is eligible?

The Quarterly Return Monthly Payment (QRMP) scheme allows taxpayers with aggregate turnover up to Rs. 5 crore to file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B quarterly instead of monthly. However, tax payment remains monthly through Form GST PMT-06, due by the 25th of the following month. You can opt in or out of QRMP at the beginning of each quarter on the GST portal.

What happens if I miss my GST return filing deadline?

Missing a GST deadline triggers multiple consequences: late fees starting from Rs. 20–50 per day, interest at 18% per annum on outstanding tax, inability to file subsequent returns until the missed one is cleared, blocking of ITC for your buyers, potential suspension of e-way bill generation after two consecutive missed filings, and possible cancellation of your GST registration after six months of non-compliance.
