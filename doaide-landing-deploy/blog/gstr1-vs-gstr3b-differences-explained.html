GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B: Key Differences Explained

Published on October 6, 2026 • 13 min read • Updated for FY 2026-27

Every regular GST taxpayer in India must file two primary returns — GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B — every month or every quarter. While both returns deal with your business's outward supplies and tax obligations, they serve fundamentally different purposes and contain different levels of detail. Understanding these differences is critical for accurate GST compliance and avoiding costly mismatches that can trigger scrutiny from the tax department.

This guide provides a thorough, side-by-side comparison of GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, explains how the two returns interact with each other and with GSTR-2B, breaks down every table in both returns, covers the most common reconciliation issues, and offers practical strategies to keep your returns aligned.

Whether you are a business owner preparing your own returns, a CA managing multiple clients, or an accounts team member responsible for GST compliance, this guide will give you the clarity you need to file both returns accurately and confidently.

Quick Overview: What Are GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B?

GSTR-1: Statement of Outward Supplies

GSTR-1 is a detailed statement that captures all outward supplies (sales) made by a registered taxpayer during a given tax period. It includes invoice-level details for B2B transactions, consolidated data for B2C transactions, credit and debit notes, exports, and amendments to previously reported invoices. GSTR-1 does not involve any tax payment — it is purely an information return.

The primary importance of GSTR-1 lies in its downstream impact: the data you report in GSTR-1 automatically flows into your buyers' GSTR-2B, which determines the input tax credit (ITC) they can claim. If you file GSTR-1 late or with errors, your buyers cannot claim their legitimate ITC, which directly affects their business.

GSTR-3B: Self-Assessed Summary Return

GSTR-3B is a self-assessed summary return where you declare your total tax liability from outward supplies, claim input tax credit from inward supplies, report reverse charge liability, and make the actual tax payment. Unlike GSTR-1's invoice-level granularity, GSTR-3B uses consolidated figures grouped by supply type and tax rate.

GSTR-3B is the return where money changes hands. Your tax liability is computed here, ITC is utilized here, and the net tax payable is remitted through the electronic cash ledger. It is also the return that determines your compliance status — failing to file GSTR-3B has the most immediate consequences, including e-way bill blocking and interest on unpaid tax.

How GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B Are Connected

Understanding the relationship between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B is fundamental to avoiding mismatches and ensuring smooth compliance. Here is how the two returns interact:

Data Flow: GSTR-1 to GSTR-2B to GSTR-3B

The GST ecosystem follows a specific data flow pattern:

  1. You file GSTR-1 with your outward supply details (invoice data for sales)
  2. System generates GSTR-2B for your buyers — your GSTR-1 invoices appear as inward supplies in your buyers' GSTR-2B, determining the ITC available to them
  3. Your buyers file GSTR-3B — they claim ITC in Table 4 based on what appears in their GSTR-2B (which came from your GSTR-1)
  4. You file GSTR-3B — you declare consolidated output tax (which should match your GSTR-1 totals) and claim your own ITC (based on your GSTR-2B from your suppliers' GSTR-1)

This circular dependency means that every participant's GSTR-1 accuracy directly affects other participants' GSTR-3B accuracy. A single error in your GSTR-1 can cascade through the entire supply chain.

Filing Sequence: Why GSTR-1 Should Come First

While the GST portal does not technically mandate filing GSTR-1 before GSTR-3B, there are compelling reasons to always file GSTR-1 first:

1. Auto-Population Accuracy

When you file GSTR-1 first, the output tax figures in GSTR-3B are auto-populated from your GSTR-1 data. This serves as a built-in verification — if the auto-populated figures match your books, you have confidence in the accuracy of both returns. If they don't match, you can investigate before filing GSTR-3B.

2. ITC for Your Buyers

Your GSTR-1 data flows into your buyers' GSTR-2B. If you file GSTR-1 late, your buyers cannot see your invoices in their GSTR-2B and may not be able to claim the full ITC when they file their GSTR-3B. This can damage business relationships and force your buyers to either defer their ITC or claim it provisionally with risks.

3. Avoiding DRC-01B Notices

The system cross-checks GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B. If you file GSTR-3B first with a lower tax liability and then file GSTR-1 showing higher sales, the system may issue a DRC-01B notice for the positive difference. Filing GSTR-1 first allows you to reconcile before committing to GSTR-3B figures.

4. Reconciliation Opportunity

Between the GSTR-1 due date (11th) and the GSTR-3B due date (20th), you have a 9-day window to reconcile your output tax (from GSTR-1), your input tax credit (from GSTR-2B generated on the 14th), and finalize your GSTR-3B. This window is wasted if you file both on the same day.

Common Reconciliation Issues Between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B

Mismatches between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B are one of the most common compliance issues faced by Indian businesses. Here are the most frequent causes and how to resolve them.

1. Turnover Mismatch

The total taxable turnover reported in GSTR-1 (sum of Tables 4, 5, 6, 7) should match the taxable turnover in GSTR-3B Table 3.1(a). Common causes of mismatch include:

  • Advances received — Tax on advances received (where the invoice is not yet issued) is reportable in GSTR-3B but may not be captured in GSTR-1 until the invoice is raised.
  • Credit notes — Credit notes filed in GSTR-1 reduce the output in GSTR-1 but may not be adjusted in the corresponding GSTR-3B period.
  • Debit notes — Debit notes reported in GSTR-1 increase the output but may be omitted from GSTR-3B Table 3.1.
  • Amendments — Amendments in Tables 9-11 of GSTR-1 change the net output of a previous period but are sometimes not reflected in GSTR-3B.
  • Rounding differences — Minor rounding differences between invoice-level reporting (GSTR-1) and consolidated reporting (GSTR-3B).

2. Tax Amount Mismatch

Even when turnover matches, the tax amounts can differ due to:

  • Incorrect tax rate classification — An item reported at 18% in GSTR-1 but at 12% in GSTR-3B (or vice versa).
  • IGST vs CGST+SGST misclassification — Reporting an intra-state supply as inter-state (IGST instead of CGST+SGST) in one return but correctly in the other.
  • Exempt supply reporting — Incorrectly including exempt supplies in the taxable supplies of one return but not the other.
  • Cess miscalculation — Forgetting to include compensation cess in one of the returns.

3. B2B vs B2C Classification Errors

In GSTR-1, B2B and B2C supplies are reported in different tables with different levels of detail. However, in GSTR-3B Table 3.1, they are consolidated together. Errors can arise when:

  • An invoice issued with GSTIN (B2B) is reported in the B2C tables of GSTR-1
  • An inter-state B2C supply above Rs 2.5 lakh is reported in Table 7 (B2C Small) instead of Table 5 (B2C Large)
  • Table 3.1.1 of GSTR-3B (inter-state B2C breakdown) does not match GSTR-1 Tables 5 and 7

4. Export and Zero-Rated Supply Differences

Export invoices can cause mismatches when:

  • Exports with payment of IGST are reported in GSTR-1 Table 6 but the IGST is omitted from GSTR-3B Table 3.1(b)
  • Exports without payment (under bond/LUT) are reported differently in GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B
  • SEZ supplies are classified differently between the two returns
  • Deemed exports are included in one return but not the other

5. Reverse Charge Differences

Reverse charge mechanism (RCM) supplies can be tricky because they appear in different places:

AspectGSTR-1 TreatmentGSTR-3B Treatment
Outward supplies on RCM by supplierNot reported in GSTR-1 (it's the recipient's liability)Not reported in supplier's GSTR-3B
Inward supplies on RCM by recipientNot reported in recipient's GSTR-1Reported in Table 3.1(d) of recipient's GSTR-3B
ITC on RCM suppliesN/AClaimed in Table 4 after paying tax in Table 3.1(d)

The confusion arises because RCM supplies are a GSTR-3B-only item — they do not appear in any GSTR-1 table. Including them in GSTR-1 or excluding them from GSTR-3B are common errors.

6. Credit and Debit Note Timing Differences

Credit notes and debit notes have time limits and conditions under Section 34. A frequent reconciliation issue occurs when:

  • A credit note is issued and reported in GSTR-1 for period X, but the corresponding reduction in output tax is adjusted in GSTR-3B for period Y
  • A financial credit note (without GST impact) is mistakenly reported in GSTR-1 as a tax credit note
  • Credit notes beyond the time limit (September of the following financial year or the date of filing annual return, whichever is earlier) are reported in GSTR-1 but should not reduce tax liability in GSTR-3B

Form DRC-01B: The Mismatch Notice

The government introduced Form DRC-01B to systemically address GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B mismatches. Understanding this form is crucial for every taxpayer.

When Is DRC-01B Issued?

The system generates a DRC-01B notice when:

  • The tax liability declared in GSTR-1/IFF for a period exceeds the tax paid in GSTR-3B for the same period
  • The difference crosses a specified threshold (currently, the threshold is being adjusted periodically by the government)
  • The mismatch is a positive difference — meaning you reported more tax in GSTR-1 than you paid in GSTR-3B

What Happens After DRC-01B Is Issued?

  1. You receive the notice on your GST portal dashboard and via registered email
  2. You must respond within 7 days
  3. You can either:
    • Pay the differential amount by filing Form DRC-03 (creating a challan for the difference)
    • Provide an explanation detailing why the mismatch exists (legitimate reasons like credit notes, amendments, advances adjustment, etc.)
  4. If you do not respond within 7 days, the system may restrict your ability to file the next GSTR-1

Prevention is better than cure: The best way to avoid DRC-01B notices is to reconcile your GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B before filing. Ensure that the total output tax in your GSTR-1 (after adjustments for credit notes and amendments) matches or is less than the tax declared and paid in your GSTR-3B.

Impact on Input Tax Credit

The relationship between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B has a direct impact on the entire ITC ecosystem. Here is how:

Supplier's GSTR-1 Determines Buyer's ITC

Under Section 16(2)(aa) of the CGST Act, a buyer can claim ITC only if the invoice details appear in their GSTR-2B. Since GSTR-2B is generated from the supplier's GSTR-1, any of the following issues with the supplier's GSTR-1 will block the buyer's ITC:

  • Supplier files GSTR-1 late — buyer's GSTR-2B for that month does not include the invoice
  • Supplier reports wrong GSTIN — invoice appears in the wrong buyer's GSTR-2B
  • Supplier reports wrong tax amount — buyer sees a different ITC figure than expected
  • Supplier does not file GSTR-1 at all — invoice never appears in any GSTR-2B

ITC Matching: GSTR-2B vs GSTR-3B Table 4

When filing GSTR-3B, Table 4 (Eligible ITC) is auto-populated from your GSTR-2B. The ITC you claim should ideally match or be less than what GSTR-2B shows. Claiming ITC that exceeds GSTR-2B can trigger scrutiny under Rule 36(4), which provides that ITC on invoices not reflected in GSTR-2B can be claimed only to a specified extent (currently, the provisional credit is being progressively restricted).

  1. Maintain a real-time sales register — Keep your sales register updated daily with invoice details, so that GSTR-1 preparation is a matter of export and upload rather than a month-end scramble.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B?

GSTR-1 is a detailed statement of outward supplies (sales) containing invoice-level information, while GSTR-3B is a summary self-assessed return where you declare consolidated tax liability, claim input tax credit, and make the actual tax payment. GSTR-1 reports what you sold; GSTR-3B determines how much tax you pay.

Should I file GSTR-1 or GSTR-3B first?

You should always file GSTR-1 first. While the GST portal technically allows filing GSTR-3B before GSTR-1, filing GSTR-1 first ensures data consistency, triggers auto-population of GSTR-3B, and allows your buyers to claim ITC from their GSTR-2B. The ideal filing window is GSTR-1 by the 11th, then reconcile with GSTR-2B (available on 14th), then file GSTR-3B by the 20th.

What happens if there is a mismatch between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B?

Mismatches can trigger a Form DRC-01B notice from the GST system, requiring you to explain the difference or pay the differential tax within 7 days. If not resolved, the system may restrict your ability to file subsequent GSTR-1. Persistent mismatches can also lead to demand notices under Section 73 or 74 with penalties.

Does GSTR-1 data auto-populate in GSTR-3B?

Yes, certain GSTR-3B fields are now auto-populated from GSTR-1 data, particularly Table 3.1 (outward supplies) and Table 3.2 (inter-state B2C). However, GSTR-3B remains a self-assessed return — you can modify the auto-populated figures, but significant deviations may trigger DRC-01B notices.

Can I revise GSTR-1 or GSTR-3B after filing?

Neither GSTR-1 nor GSTR-3B can be revised once filed. GSTR-1 corrections are made through amendment tables (9, 10, 11) in the next period. GSTR-3B adjustments are made in subsequent periods — under-reported tax is paid with interest, and over-reported tax is adjusted. This makes accuracy at filing time critical.

What is GSTR-2B and how does it relate to GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B?

GSTR-2B is an auto-generated ITC statement for buyers, created from their suppliers' GSTR-1 data. It is available on the 14th of each month. Your GSTR-1 invoices appear in your buyers' GSTR-2B, and your own GSTR-2B (from your suppliers' GSTR-1) determines the ITC you can claim in your GSTR-3B Table 4.

Why does the turnover in my GSTR-1 not match GSTR-3B?

Common causes include: advances received but not yet invoiced (in GSTR-3B but not GSTR-1), credit notes in GSTR-1 not reflected in GSTR-3B, amendments affecting one return but not the other, rounding differences, and different treatment of reverse charge supplies. Monthly reconciliation using a GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B comparison sheet prevents these mismatches.

Is GSTR-1 mandatory for composition scheme taxpayers?

No, composition scheme taxpayers do not file GSTR-1 or GSTR-3B. They file CMP-08 (quarterly statement-cum-challan) and GSTR-4 (annual return) instead. The GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B filing regime applies exclusively to regular GST taxpayers.

What is Form DRC-01B and when is it issued?

Form DRC-01B is an automatic notice issued when tax liability in GSTR-1 significantly exceeds the tax paid in GSTR-3B for the same period. You must respond within 7 days by either paying the differential amount or explaining the discrepancy. Failure to respond may restrict your subsequent GSTR-1 filing.

How does the Invoice Furnishing Facility (IFF) affect the GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B relationship?

IFF allows QRMP scheme taxpayers to upload B2B invoices monthly for the first two months of each quarter. These invoices flow into buyers' GSTR-2B and are automatically included in the quarterly GSTR-1 — they do not need to be re-reported. IFF does not change the GSTR-3B filing; tax payments continue via monthly PMT-06 and quarterly GSTR-3B.
