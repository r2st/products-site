If your business has purchased machinery, computers, vehicles, or any other asset that gets capitalized in your books of accounts, you are likely eligible to claim Input Tax Credit (ITC) on capital goods under GST. However, the rules around claiming this credit are nuanced. Getting them wrong can lead to excess ITC reversals, interest liability, or penalties during GST audits.

This guide covers everything you need to know about input tax credit on capital goods under GST in 2026 -- from the legal definition and eligibility conditions to the detailed reversal formula under Rule 43, blocked credits, disposal rules, and the mistakes that trip up even experienced accountants.

1. What Are Capital Goods Under GST?

Section 2(19) of the CGST Act, 2017 defines capital goods as goods whose value is capitalised in the books of accounts of the person claiming ITC, and which are used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business.

This is a significant departure from the pre-GST era, where capital goods had a much narrower definition tied to specific tariff headings. Under GST, any good that meets two conditions qualifies as a capital good:

  1. Its value is capitalised in the books of accounts -- meaning it is recorded as an asset rather than an expense.
  2. It is used or intended to be used in the course or furtherance of business.
Key Point

The definition is broad and asset-class-agnostic. Whether it is a CNC machine worth Rs. 50 lakh or a laptop worth Rs. 45,000, if you capitalise it in your books and use it for business, it qualifies as a capital good for ITC purposes.

Note that plant and machinery is explicitly excluded from the definition of "immovable property" under the CGST Act, which means you can claim ITC on plant and machinery even though ITC on immovable property (buildings, civil structures) is generally blocked.

3. Eligibility Conditions for Claiming ITC on Capital Goods

To successfully claim ITC on capital goods, you must satisfy all of the following conditions laid down in Section 16 of the CGST Act:

3.1 GST Registration

You must be a registered person under GST. Composition scheme dealers and unregistered persons cannot claim ITC. If you obtained registration after purchasing the capital good, you can claim ITC on goods held in stock on the date of registration, subject to the condition that the invoice is not older than one year (Section 18(1)).

3.2 Use for Taxable Supplies

The capital goods must be used or intended to be used for making taxable supplies (including zero-rated supplies such as exports). If capital goods are used exclusively for making exempt supplies or for personal purposes, no ITC is available. If used partly for taxable and partly for exempt supplies, proportionate ITC must be reversed under Rule 43.

3.3 Valid Tax Invoice or Debit Note

You must possess a valid tax invoice or debit note issued by the supplier. The invoice must contain your correct GSTIN, the HSN code of the goods, the applicable tax rate, and the tax amount. Self-invoices are acceptable for reverse charge purchases.

3.4 Supplier Must Have Filed GSTR-1

The supplier must have uploaded the invoice details in their GSTR-1 return, and these details must appear in your GSTR-2B. This is the auto-populated statement that the GST portal generates for you. If the invoice does not appear in your GSTR-2B, the ITC will be treated as provisional and may be denied during assessment.

3.5 Receipt of Goods

The goods must have been received by you. For goods delivered in instalments, ITC can be claimed only when the last instalment is received. If the goods are delivered to a third party on your direction (bill-to-ship-to), receipt by the third party is considered receipt by you.

3.6 Payment Within 180 Days

You must make payment to the supplier within 180 days from the date of the invoice. If payment is not made within this period, you must reverse the ITC claimed, along with interest. The ITC can be re-claimed once payment is eventually made.

3.7 No Double Benefit with Depreciation

Important

Under Section 16(3) of the CGST Act, if you claim depreciation on the tax component of a capital good under the Income Tax Act, 1961, you cannot claim ITC on the same tax amount. You must choose one benefit -- ITC or depreciation on the GST portion -- not both.

In practice, claiming ITC is almost always more beneficial than claiming depreciation on the GST component, because ITC gives you an immediate credit against your GST liability, whereas depreciation spreads the benefit over several years.

4. How to Claim ITC on Capital Goods (Step by Step)

One of the most significant improvements under GST compared to the old excise and service tax regime is the elimination of the 50% restriction. Under the earlier CENVAT Credit Rules, you could claim only 50% of the ITC on capital goods in the first year, and the remaining 50% in the subsequent year.

Under GST, you can claim 100% of the ITC on capital goods in the tax period in which the goods are received.

Here is the step-by-step process:

  1. Receive the capital goods at your place of business (or at the location of a third party on your instructions).
  2. Verify the tax invoice -- ensure your GSTIN, the HSN code, tax rate, and tax amounts are correct.
  3. Capitalise the asset in your books of accounts (excluding the GST component if you are claiming ITC).
  4. Check GSTR-2B -- confirm that the supplier has uploaded the invoice in their GSTR-1 and it is reflected in your GSTR-2B auto-populated statement.
  5. Claim ITC in GSTR-3B -- report the full ITC amount in Table 4A of your GSTR-3B return for that tax period.
  6. Maintain records -- keep the invoice, delivery challan, goods receipt note, and capitalization entry for at least 72 months (6 years) after the due date of the annual return for the relevant year.
Example

Your business purchases a CNC machine for Rs. 10,00,000 + 18% GST (Rs. 1,80,000) in July 2026. The machine is received and the supplier has uploaded the invoice in GSTR-1. You can claim the entire Rs. 1,80,000 as ITC in your July 2026 GSTR-3B return. The machine should be capitalised at Rs. 10,00,000 in your books (not Rs. 11,80,000).

5. ITC Reversal Rules: Rule 43 of CGST Rules

When capital goods are used partly for taxable supplies and partly for exempt supplies (or partly for business and partly for personal purposes), you cannot retain the full ITC. Rule 43 of the CGST Rules prescribes a detailed mechanism for proportionate reversal.

5.1 Step 1: Determine the Total ITC (Tc)

When you receive the capital goods, claim the full ITC. The total ITC on a capital good is called Tc (Total Common Credit for that asset).

However, if the capital good is used exclusively for exempt supplies or personal purposes, no ITC can be claimed at all. If used exclusively for taxable supplies, the full ITC is available with no reversal needed. Rule 43 applies only to capital goods used for both taxable and exempt supplies (common capital goods).

5.2 Step 2: Useful Life and Monthly Credit

The useful life of capital goods is deemed to be 60 months (5 years) from the date of invoice. The ITC is spread across these 60 months for reversal calculation purposes.

Monthly Common Credit = Tc / 60

5.3 Step 3: Calculate the Reversal Amount

For each tax period, calculate the amount of ITC to be reversed using this formula:

ITC to Reverse = (Tc / 60) x (Exempt Turnover / Total Turnover)

Where:

  • Tc = Total ITC on the common capital good
  • Exempt Turnover = Value of exempt supplies during the tax period
  • Total Turnover = Value of all supplies (taxable + exempt + zero-rated) during the tax period

Important: Any part of a quarter is treated as a full quarter for the 60-month calculation.

5.4 Worked Example

Example: Rule 43 Reversal Calculation

Facts: A business purchases an industrial oven for Rs. 6,00,000 + GST at 18% = Rs. 1,08,000 ITC. The oven is used to make both taxable baked goods and exempt unbranded food items.

In a given month:

  • Taxable turnover: Rs. 8,00,000
  • Exempt turnover: Rs. 2,00,000
  • Total turnover: Rs. 10,00,000

Calculation:

Monthly common credit = Rs. 1,08,000 / 60 = Rs. 1,800

ITC to reverse this month = Rs. 1,800 x (2,00,000 / 10,00,000) = Rs. 1,800 x 0.20 = Rs. 360

This Rs. 360 must be added to the output tax liability for that month. The reversal is reported in Table 4(B) of GSTR-3B.

5.5 Annual True-Up

At the end of the financial year, you must recalculate the reversal using the actual annual turnover figures (instead of monthly figures). Any difference between the monthly reversals already made and the annual recalculation must be adjusted -- either as additional reversal or as re-credit. This annual reconciliation is typically done in the GSTR-9 annual return.

6. Blocked ITC on Capital Goods: Section 17(5)

Even if all eligibility conditions are met, ITC is blocked on certain categories of capital goods under Section 17(5) of the CGST Act. The most significant ones are:

6.1 Motor Vehicles (Seating Capacity up to 13 Persons)

ITC on motor vehicles with a seating capacity of up to 13 persons (including the driver) is blocked. This covers cars, SUVs, and most passenger vehicles purchased for business use.

Exceptions where ITC IS available:

  • Vehicles used for transportation of passengers (e.g., cab services, tour operators)
  • Vehicles used for transportation of goods (trucks, tempos, goods carriers)
  • Vehicles used for imparting driving training
  • Vehicles that are the stock-in-trade of the business (car dealerships, vehicle rental companies)

6.2 Vessels and Aircraft

ITC on vessels and aircraft is blocked unless used for making taxable supplies of transportation or for training purposes. Businesses in the aviation and maritime transport industry can claim ITC; others cannot.

6.3 Goods Used for Personal Consumption

If capital goods are used for personal consumption of the owner, promoter, director, or employees, ITC is not available. For example, a laptop provided to the director for exclusively personal use would not be eligible for ITC.

6.4 Goods Used for Making Exempt Supplies

Capital goods used exclusively for exempt supplies do not qualify for ITC. If a hospital buys an X-ray machine and uses it exclusively for healthcare services (which are exempt under GST), no ITC is available.

Practical Tip

When you purchase a vehicle for business, evaluate carefully whether your use case falls under one of the exceptions. A delivery vehicle used for transporting goods qualifies for full ITC. A sedan used by the MD for commuting does not.

7. ITC on Disposal or Sale of Capital Goods Before Useful Life Ends

If you sell, discard, or otherwise dispose of capital goods before the end of their 5-year useful life, Section 18(6) read with Rule 44(6) requires you to pay back a portion of the ITC.

The amount payable is the higher of:

  1. ITC attributable to the remaining useful life: Calculated as (ITC claimed / 60) multiplied by the number of months remaining out of 60. Any part of a quarter counts as a full quarter.
  2. GST on the transaction value: The GST applicable on the price at which the capital good is sold.
Example: Disposal Calculation

Facts: A machine was purchased in April 2024 with ITC of Rs. 1,80,000. It is sold in October 2026 for Rs. 4,00,000 (30 months of use).

Option A (Remaining life method):

Remaining months = 60 - 30 = 30 months

ITC to pay back = Rs. 1,80,000 x (30/60) = Rs. 90,000

Option B (Transaction value method):

GST on sale = 18% x Rs. 4,00,000 = Rs. 72,000

Amount payable = Rs. 90,000 (higher of Rs. 90,000 and Rs. 72,000)

This amount must be paid via Form GST DRC-03 or adjusted in the GSTR-3B return.

If capital goods are completely written off, destroyed, or lost, the same provisions apply. You must reverse the ITC proportionate to the remaining useful life. The only exception is when goods are destroyed by natural calamity and duly certified -- in which case the reversal may not be required (subject to conditions).

8. Common Mistakes Businesses Make When Claiming ITC on Capital Goods

Based on patterns observed in GST audits and assessments, here are the most frequent errors businesses make with ITC on capital goods:

  1. Claiming ITC and depreciation on the same GST amount. This is the most common error. The Income Tax team capitalises the asset including GST, claiming depreciation on the full amount, while the GST team claims ITC on the same tax. This double benefit is explicitly prohibited under Section 16(3) and will be caught during assessment.
  2. Not matching invoices with GSTR-2B. Many businesses claim ITC based on invoices received without checking whether the supplier has uploaded the invoice. Mismatches lead to notices under Section 73/74.
  3. Forgetting Rule 43 reversals for mixed-use assets. If your capital goods serve both taxable and exempt supplies, the monthly reversal under Rule 43 is mandatory. Skipping this creates a cumulative liability with interest at 18% per annum.
  4. Claiming ITC on blocked categories. Businesses sometimes claim ITC on cars used by directors, or on office construction, not realising these fall under Section 17(5). The ITC is denied during audit with interest and a 10-15% penalty.
  5. Not reversing ITC on disposal. When capital goods are sold, scrapped, or written off, the ITC reversal under Section 18(6) is frequently missed. This is especially common when different teams handle asset disposal and GST compliance.
  6. Incorrect useful life calculation. Some businesses use the Income Tax useful life (varying by asset class) instead of the GST deemed useful life of 60 months. The GST useful life is always 60 months regardless of the asset class.
  7. Missing the 180-day payment window. For high-value capital goods purchased on credit terms, the 180-day payment deadline can slip through. If payment is not made within this period, ITC must be reversed and can only be re-claimed upon payment.
  8. Not claiming ITC on capital goods held in stock at the time of GST registration. Businesses that register under GST after purchasing capital goods often do not know they can claim ITC on goods held in stock on the date of registration, provided the invoice is not older than one year.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I claim full ITC on capital goods in the first year itself?
Yes. Under GST, you can claim 100% of the ITC on capital goods in the tax period in which you receive the goods. The old 50% restriction from the CENVAT Credit Rules no longer applies. The only condition is that all eligibility requirements under Section 16 are met.
What is the useful life of capital goods for ITC reversal purposes?
Under GST (Rule 43 and Rule 44), the useful life of capital goods is deemed to be 60 months (5 years) from the date of the invoice. This is a fixed period and does not vary by asset class -- unlike the Income Tax Act where useful life differs for different types of assets.
Can I claim ITC on a car purchased for business use?
Generally, no. ITC on motor vehicles with seating capacity up to 13 persons is blocked under Section 17(5). Exceptions apply if the vehicle is used for transportation of passengers (cab/taxi business), transportation of goods, driving training, or if vehicles are your stock-in-trade (dealerships, rental companies).
What happens to ITC if I sell capital goods before 5 years?
Under Section 18(6), you must pay back the higher of: (a) ITC proportionate to the remaining useful life out of 60 months, or (b) the GST payable on the transaction value of the sale. This amount is added to your output tax liability.
Can I claim both ITC and depreciation on the GST amount?
No. Section 16(3) explicitly prohibits claiming both benefits on the same tax amount. You must choose either ITC under GST or depreciation on the tax component under the Income Tax Act. In most cases, claiming ITC provides a larger and more immediate benefit.
How does Rule 43 work for mixed-use capital goods?
Rule 43 requires you to first claim the full ITC. Then, each month, reverse ITC proportionate to exempt supplies: (Tc / 60) multiplied by (Exempt Turnover / Total Turnover). At year-end, recalculate using actual annual figures and adjust any difference. This continues for the full 60-month useful life.
Can I claim ITC on capital goods purchased before GST registration?
Yes, under Section 18(1). When you obtain GST registration, you can claim ITC on capital goods held in stock on the date of registration, provided the invoice is not older than one year from the date of registration. The ITC is reduced by 5% per quarter (or part thereof) from the date of invoice to the date of registration.
Is ITC available on second-hand capital goods?
ITC is available if the seller charges GST on the sale. If the seller is a registered dealer selling used goods under the margin scheme (Rule 32(5)), GST is charged only on the margin (sale price minus depreciated value), and ITC is available only on that GST amount. If the seller is unregistered, no ITC is available.

Conclusion

Claiming ITC on capital goods under GST is one of the most impactful ways for businesses to reduce their effective tax cost. Unlike the old regime, GST allows full credit in the period of receipt, making the cash flow benefit immediate and substantial. However, the rules around reversal (Rule 43), blocked credits (Section 17(5)), and disposal (Section 18(6)) require careful attention.

The key takeaways for 2026:

  • Claim full ITC in the month of receipt -- no 50% restriction.
  • Always check GSTR-2B before claiming.
  • Never claim both ITC and depreciation on the GST component.
  • Perform Rule 43 reversals monthly for mixed-use assets and reconcile annually.
  • Remember the 60-month useful life for all disposal and reversal calculations.
  • Verify that your capital goods do not fall under Section 17(5) blocked categories.
