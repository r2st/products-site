Understanding Indian Salary Components

When you receive a job offer in India, the CTC (Cost to Company) figure can be very different from what you actually take home every month. Here's a breakdown of the key components:

Basic Salary

Basic salary is the core component of your pay structure, typically 40–50% of CTC. It forms the basis for calculating HRA, PF, and gratuity. A higher basic means more PF savings but also higher tax liability since basic salary is fully taxable.

House Rent Allowance (HRA)

HRA is provided to help employees pay rent. Under the Old Tax Regime, you can claim HRA exemption — the minimum of: actual HRA received, 50% of basic (metro) or 40% (non-metro), or rent paid minus 10% of basic. The New Regime does not allow HRA exemption.

Provident Fund (PF)

Both employer and employee contribute 12% of basic salary (capped at ₹15,000 basic per month for the employer's mandatory contribution). Employee PF is deducted from your salary. Employer PF is part of CTC but does not come to you as cash — it goes into your PF account.

Professional Tax

Professional tax is a state-level tax on salaried individuals. The maximum is ₹2,500 per year. Most states charge ₹200/month (₹2,400/year). This calculator uses ₹2,400 as a standard deduction applicable in most states.

Income Tax

Your salary income is taxed based on the regime you choose. The New Tax Regime is the default from FY 2024-25 onward. You can opt for the Old Regime if it benefits you more (typically when you have significant deductions).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is CTC and how is it different from take-home salary?
CTC (Cost to Company) is the total amount a company spends on an employee per year. It includes basic salary, HRA, special allowances, employer PF contribution, gratuity, and other benefits. Take-home salary (in-hand salary) is what you actually receive after deductions like employee PF, professional tax, and income tax.
Which tax regime is better — Old or New for FY 2026-27?
The New Tax Regime offers lower tax rates and a higher basic exemption limit (₹4 lakh vs ₹2.5 lakh) but does not allow most deductions like 80C, HRA, or home loan interest. The Old Regime has higher rates but allows deductions. If your total deductions exceed ₹3–4 lakh, the Old Regime may save more tax. Use our calculator above to compare both side-by-side with your actual numbers.
How is HRA exemption calculated?
HRA exemption under the Old Tax Regime is the minimum of: (1) Actual HRA received, (2) 50% of basic salary for metro cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata) or 40% for non-metro cities, and (3) Actual rent paid minus 10% of basic salary. You need rent receipts to claim this exemption.
What is the standard deduction for salaried employees?
Under the New Tax Regime, the standard deduction is ₹75,000 for FY 2026-27. Under the Old Tax Regime, it is ₹50,000. This is a flat deduction from salary income — no bills or proofs required.
Is employer PF part of my take-home salary?
No. Employer PF (12% of basic, capped at ₹1,800/month on ₹15,000 basic) is part of your CTC but goes directly into your EPF account. It's a retirement benefit, not cash in hand. Employee PF (your 12% contribution) is also deducted from your salary.
How accurate is this calculator?
This calculator provides a close estimate based on standard salary structures. Actual take-home may vary based on your company's specific CTC breakup, additional perks (food coupons, car lease, etc.), and state-specific professional tax rates. For exact figures, refer to your salary slip or HR department.
