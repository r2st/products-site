E-Way Bill Under GST: Rules, Validity & Exemptions

Every goods movement above ₹50,000 needs an e-way bill. Miss one, and the goods and vehicle can be detained with a penalty equal to the tax amount. Here's everything you need to know.

The e-way bill system, introduced under GST in April 2018, is an electronic document required for the movement of goods worth more than ₹50,000. It's designed to track goods in transit and prevent tax evasion — but for small businesses, understanding when you need one (and when you don't) can save hours of compliance work and thousands in penalties.

What Is an E-Way Bill?

An e-way bill (Electronic Way Bill) is a document generated on the GST e-way bill portal that must accompany goods during transportation. It contains details about the consignment — the goods, their value, the supplier, the recipient, and the transporter.

Every e-way bill has two parts:

  • Part A: Goods details — GSTIN of supplier and recipient, place of delivery, document number, value, HSN code, and reason for transport
  • Part B: Transport details — vehicle number or transport document number. Required only when goods move by road for more than 50 km

When Is an E-Way Bill Required?

An e-way bill must be generated when goods with a consignment value exceeding ₹50,000 are moved:

  • In relation to a supply (sale)
  • For reasons other than supply (job work, inter-branch transfer, exhibition)
  • Due to inward supply from an unregistered person

Important

The ₹50,000 threshold applies to the consignment value (total of all invoices in the consignment), not the individual invoice value. If you're sending 3 invoices worth ₹20,000 each in a single vehicle, the consignment value is ₹60,000 — and an e-way bill is required.

E-Way Bill Validity Period

The validity of an e-way bill depends on the distance and vehicle type:

Vehicle TypeDistanceValidity
Regular vehicleUp to 200 km1 day
Regular vehicleEvery additional 200 km+1 day
Over-dimensional cargo (ODC)Up to 20 km1 day
Over-dimensional cargo (ODC)Every additional 20 km+1 day

Validity starts from the date and time of e-way bill generation. For example:

  • Mumbai to Pune (150 km, regular vehicle) → 1-day validity
  • Delhi to Jaipur (280 km, regular vehicle) → 2-day validity
  • Chennai to Bengaluru (350 km, regular vehicle) → 2-day validity
  • Mumbai to Kolkata (2,000 km, regular vehicle) → 10-day validity

Extending Validity

If goods cannot reach the destination within the validity period (due to breakdown, bad weather, law & order issues, or transshipment delay), the validity can be extended. The extension must be done 8 hours before or 8 hours after the expiry time, on the e-way bill portal.

When Part B is not required, only Part A of the e-way bill needs to be filled. This is common for intra-city deliveries under 50 km.

Goods Exempt from E-Way Bill

Certain goods do not require an e-way bill regardless of value:

  • Liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) for household use
  • Kerosene oil sold under PDS
  • Postal baggage transported by government postal services
  • Natural or cultured pearls, precious stones, and metals
  • Jewellery, goldsmiths' wares
  • Currency
  • Used personal and household effects
  • Coral, unworked
  • Empty cargo containers
  • Goods transported by non-motorized conveyance (hand carts, bullock carts)
  • Goods transported from port/airport/land customs station to an inland container depot (ICD) for customs clearance

Consolidated E-Way Bill

When a transporter carries multiple consignments in a single vehicle, they can generate a consolidated e-way bill instead of separate bills for each consignment. This clubs all individual e-way bills under one document, making roadside verification easier.

Each individual consignment still needs its own e-way bill — the consolidated bill is an additional document that wraps them together for the vehicle.

State-Specific Rules

State Variations

Some states have lower thresholds or additional requirements for intra-state movement. For example, some states require e-way bills for intra-state movement of specific goods (like gold, tobacco) regardless of value. Always check your state's GST portal for the latest rules.

Penalties for E-Way Bill Violations

Penalty Structure

Moving goods without a valid e-way bill: Penalty of ₹10,000 or tax sought to be evaded, whichever is higher (Section 129, CGST Act).

Detention of goods and vehicle: The proper officer can intercept and detain the goods and vehicle until the penalty is paid or security is furnished.

Release conditions: For taxable goods — pay penalty equal to 200% of tax payable. For exempt goods — pay penalty equal to 2% of value of goods or ₹25,000, whichever is less.

How to Generate an E-Way Bill: Step by Step

  1. Register on the portal — Visit ewaybillgst.gov.in and register using your GSTIN
  2. Login and select "Generate New" — Choose e-way bill from the left panel
  3. Fill Part A — Enter transaction type (outward/inward), sub-type, document details, item details, HSN code, and value
  4. Fill Part B — Enter vehicle number or transporter ID and document number (for rail/air/ship)
  5. Submit — The system generates a unique 12-digit EWB number
  6. Print or share — Share the EWB number with the transporter; the driver should carry a printed copy or the EWB number

Cancellation and Rejection

  • Cancellation by generator: Within 24 hours, if goods are not transported or details are wrong. Not allowed if goods have been verified in transit.
  • Rejection by recipient: The recipient can reject an e-way bill within 72 hours of generation if goods were not received or details are incorrect. If neither accepted nor rejected within 72 hours, it is deemed accepted.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Wrong vehicle number — If the vehicle changes mid-transit, update the e-way bill before the goods move on the new vehicle
  2. Expired e-way bill — Extend validity before it expires; a lapsed bill is treated as not having one
  3. Splitting invoices to stay under ₹50,000 — This is treated as tax evasion if the goods are part of a single consignment
  4. Not generating for stock transfers — E-way bills are required for inter-branch transfers even though no sale is involved
  5. Forgetting Part B — Road movements over 50 km without Part B make the e-way bill invalid

Key Takeaways

Summary

₹50,000 threshold — E-way bill required for consignment values above this. Some states have lower limits.

Validity — 1 day per 200 km for regular vehicles; 1 day per 20 km for ODC. Can be extended within 8 hours of expiry.

Part B is needed — When goods move by road for more than 50 km. Otherwise, only Part A suffices.

Penalty — ₹10,000 or the tax amount, whichever is higher, plus detention of goods and vehicle.
