GST Invoice Format & Rules: Complete Guide for Indian Businesses

Every rupee of Input Tax Credit depends on your invoice being correct. Here's exactly what the law requires — the 16 mandatory fields, HSN digit rules, e-invoicing thresholds, and the penalties for getting it wrong.

A GST invoice isn't just a bill — it's a legal document that determines whether your buyer can claim Input Tax Credit (ITC). Get even one mandatory field wrong, and their ITC claim gets rejected. Get it consistently wrong, and you're looking at notices from the tax department.

This guide covers every rule around GST invoicing as of October 2026, with practical examples for small and medium businesses.

The 16 Mandatory Fields in a GST Invoice

Rule 46 of the CGST Rules prescribes these fields for every tax invoice:

Invoice Numbering Rules

Your invoice number must be:

  • Unique — no two invoices can share the same number
  • Sequential — numbers must be in a continuous series (gaps raise red flags)
  • Maximum 16 characters — alphanumeric, with only / and - as special characters
  • Financial year specific — you can start a fresh series each year (e.g., INV/2026-27/0001)

Practical Tip

Use a format like INV/2627/0001 — it sorts chronologically, fits within the 16-character limit, and makes it obvious which financial year the invoice belongs to.

CGST + SGST vs IGST: How Place of Supply Decides

The place of supply determines which tax applies:

  • Same state (supplier and buyer in same state): Charge CGST + SGST at equal rates. Example: 18% GST = 9% CGST + 9% SGST
  • Different states (inter-state supply): Charge IGST at the full rate. Example: 18% GST = 18% IGST

Getting this wrong is a common mistake and leads to incorrect returns. Your invoice must clearly show the place of supply with the state name and code.

If your aggregate turnover in any financial year from 2017-18 onwards exceeded ₹5 crore, you must generate e-invoices via the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP). The e-invoice carries a unique Invoice Reference Number (IRN) and a QR code that buyers can verify.

Time Limit for Issuing Invoices

  • Goods: At or before the time of delivery/removal. For continuous supply: at or before the date of payment or statement
  • Services: Within 30 days of supply (45 days for banks and financial institutions)
  • Exports: Before or after shipment, but within the time prescribed by RBI for realization of foreign exchange

Common Invoice Mistakes That Trigger Notices

  1. Wrong GSTIN — even one wrong digit means the buyer's ITC is rejected
  2. Missing HSN code — mandatory since April 2021
  3. CGST/SGST instead of IGST — when supplier and buyer are in different states
  4. Invoice number gaps — sequential numbering is required; gaps trigger scrutiny
  5. Late invoicing — issuing invoices past the prescribed time limit
  6. Wrong place of supply — determines the type of tax applicable

Penalties for Invoice Non-Compliance

  • Not issuing invoice: Penalty of ₹10,000 or tax amount, whichever is higher (Section 122)
  • Issuing incorrect invoice: Penalty of ₹25,000 per invoice
  • Not generating e-invoice: Invoice treated as if not issued; ITC blocked for buyer

The penalties can stack up quickly for businesses that issue hundreds of invoices per month. Getting your invoice format right from the start saves money and compliance headaches.

Key Takeaways

Summary

16 mandatory fields must appear on every tax invoice — missing any one blocks your buyer's ITC.

E-invoicing applies to businesses with turnover > ₹5 Crore in any year since 2017-18.

Place of supply determines whether you charge CGST+SGST or IGST — get it right.
