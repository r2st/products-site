HSN & SAC Codes Under GST: What They Are & How to Find Yours

Every product and service in India has a code that determines its GST rate. Use the wrong one, and you'll charge the wrong tax, file incorrect returns, and face penalties. Here's how the system works.

HSN and SAC codes are the backbone of GST classification. They determine what rate you charge, how your returns are filed, and whether your buyer's ITC claim goes through. Yet most small businesses treat them as an afterthought — copying a competitor's code or guessing from the description.

This guide explains the system from the ground up, so you can classify your products and services correctly.

What Is an HSN Code?

HSN stands for Harmonized System of Nomenclature — an internationally standardized system developed by the World Customs Organization (WCO) to classify goods. Over 200 countries use HSN codes for customs and trade, and India adopted them for GST classification.

The HSN system organizes goods into:

  • 21 Sections — broad categories (e.g., Section I: Live Animals, Section XI: Textiles)
  • 99 Chapters (2-digit codes) — e.g., Chapter 85: Electrical machinery
  • ~1,200 Headings (4-digit codes) — e.g., 8517: Telephone sets
  • ~5,000 Subheadings (6-digit codes) — e.g., 851712: Smartphones
  • 8-digit codes — India-specific extensions for granular classification

What Is a SAC Code?

SAC stands for Services Accounting Code — India's system for classifying services under GST. While HSN codes follow international standards, SAC codes are specific to India's tax system.

SAC codes always start with 99 and follow this structure:

  • 99 — Identifies it as a service (vs goods)
  • 4-digit (e.g., 9954) — Service category (Construction services)
  • 6-digit (e.g., 995411) — Specific service (Construction of residential buildings)

Common Confusion

The turnover threshold is your aggregate turnover (all taxable supplies + exempt supplies + exports + inter-state supplies of persons with same PAN), not just the turnover for a specific product. If your total business crosses ₹5 crore, ALL your invoices need 6-digit codes.

Where HSN Codes Are Used

You'll need your HSN/SAC codes in multiple places:

  1. Tax invoices — Mandatory on every invoice (4 or 6 digits depending on turnover)
  2. GSTR-1 — HSN-wise summary of outward supplies (Table 12)
  3. GSTR-3B — While filing monthly/quarterly returns
  4. GSTR-9 — Annual return with HSN-wise details of all supplies
  5. E-way bills — Product HSN code required in Part A
  6. E-invoicing — HSN code is mandatory in the IRN schema

How to Find the Right HSN Code

Follow this process to identify the correct code:

  1. Start with the Section — Identify which of the 21 sections your product falls under (e.g., Section XVI for machinery)
  2. Find the Chapter — Narrow down to the 2-digit chapter (e.g., Chapter 85 for electrical equipment)
  3. Get the Heading — Find the 4-digit heading that describes your product (e.g., 8517 for telephones)
  4. Check the Subheading — If you need 6 digits, find the subheading (e.g., 851712 for smartphones)
  5. Verify the GST rate — Cross-check with the GST rate schedule to confirm the applicable rate

Pro Tip

If you're unsure about classification, check what HSN code your suppliers use on their invoices for the same product. You can also file a ruling application with the Authority for Advance Rulings (AAR) for a binding classification decision.

Common Classification Mistakes

  1. Using a generic code instead of a specific one — "Other articles of iron or steel" (7326) when a more specific code exists
  2. Confusing goods and services — Software delivered on physical media (goods, HSN) vs software delivered electronically (service, SAC)
  3. Ignoring composite supply rules — A package with goods and services should be classified under the principal supply
  4. Not updating after rate changes — GST Council revises rates periodically; a code's rate may change
  5. Using 2-digit codes — The minimum is 4 digits for all registered taxpayers since April 2021

Penalties for Wrong HSN Classification

  • Incorrect tax collection — If you charge 12% instead of 18%, you owe the difference plus interest (18% per annum)
  • Buyer's ITC blocked — Mismatched HSN codes between supplier and buyer returns trigger ITC rejection
  • Penalty under Section 122 — Up to ₹10,000 for incorrect invoicing, or ₹50,000 for wilful misstatement
  • GSTR-1 filing errors — Table 12 (HSN summary) won't match Table 4-9 data, triggering notices

HSN Codes in GSTR-1 and GSTR-9

Your monthly/quarterly GSTR-1 requires an HSN-wise summary in Table 12. This table must report:

  • HSN code (4 or 6 digits based on turnover)
  • Description of goods/services
  • UQC (Unit Quantity Code) — e.g., KGS, NOS, MTR
  • Total quantity
  • Total taxable value
  • Rate of tax (Integrated/Central/State tax)

Your annual GSTR-9 requires the same level of detail for the entire financial year. Getting HSN codes right during the year makes annual filing significantly easier.

Key Takeaways

Summary

HSN codes classify goods (international standard). SAC codes classify services (India-specific, start with 99).

4-digit codes are mandatory for all businesses. 6-digit codes for turnover above ₹5 crore.

Correct classification matters — it determines the GST rate, affects ITC claims, and shows up in returns and e-invoices.
