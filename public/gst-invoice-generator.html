Frequently Asked Questions

A GST invoice must contain: supplier name, address and GSTIN; a unique sequential invoice number; invoice date; recipient name, address and GSTIN (for B2B); HSN/SAC code; description, quantity and value of goods/services; taxable value and GST rate; CGST + SGST or IGST amount; total invoice value; place of supply; and signature or digital signature.
E-invoicing is mandatory for businesses with aggregate turnover exceeding ₹5 crore from August 2023. The e-invoice must be generated on the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) and includes a unique Invoice Reference Number (IRN) and QR code. This tool generates the invoice format — for e-invoicing, you'd upload the JSON to the IRP.
Businesses with turnover up to ₹5 crore must mention 4-digit HSN codes on invoices. Businesses above ₹5 crore must use 6-digit codes. For exports and imports, 8-digit HSN codes are required. Nil-rated and exempt supplies may use 2-digit chapter headings.
A Tax Invoice is issued by registered taxable persons for taxable supplies and includes GST amount separately. A Bill of Supply is issued by composition scheme dealers or for exempt supplies — it does not show GST separately. Only Tax Invoices allow the recipient to claim Input Tax Credit (ITC).
