Why You Need Rent Receipts for HRA

HRA Exemption Rules (Section 10(13A))

If you receive House Rent Allowance (HRA) as part of your salary and pay rent, you can claim HRA exemption to reduce your taxable income. The exemption amount is the lowest of: actual HRA received, 50% of basic salary (metro) or 40% (non-metro), or rent paid minus 10% of basic salary.

When Are Rent Receipts Mandatory?

Rent receipts are mandatory when monthly rent exceeds Rs 3,000. Your employer requires them during the investment proof submission window (usually January-March). Without receipts, your employer will tax the full HRA component, resulting in higher TDS.

Landlord PAN Requirement

If your total annual rent exceeds Rs 1,00,000 (Rs 1 lakh), you must provide the landlord's PAN number on the rent receipts. If the landlord doesn't have a PAN, obtain a signed declaration from them stating the same. Failure to provide PAN may result in denial of HRA exemption.

How to Claim HRA Exemption

  1. Generate rent receipts: Use DoAide to create rent receipts for each month you paid rent during the financial year.
  2. Calculate HRA exemption: Use the HRA calculator to find your exemption amount. It's the lowest of: actual HRA, 50/40% of basic, or rent minus 10% of basic.
  3. Collect landlord PAN: If annual rent exceeds Rs 1 lakh, ensure you have the landlord's PAN number on all receipts.
  4. Submit to employer: During proof submission window (Jan-Mar), submit rent receipts, rent agreement copy, and landlord PAN declaration to your HR/payroll team.
  5. Verify in Form 16: After the financial year ends, verify that HRA exemption is correctly reflected in your Form 16 before filing ITR.
