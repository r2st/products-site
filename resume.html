What is an ATS and Why Does It Matter?

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software used by employers to manage the hiring process. Over 98% of Fortune 500 companies and 75% of all employers use some form of ATS. These systems automatically scan, parse, and rank resumes before a human recruiter ever sees them.

If your resume isn't optimized for ATS, it could be filtered out before reaching a hiring manager — even if you're perfectly qualified for the role. Understanding how ATS works is the first step to getting more interviews.

How ATS Systems Parse Your Resume

ATS software extracts text from your resume and looks for specific information: contact details, work experience, education, skills, and certifications. It then compares these against the job description to calculate a relevance score. Resumes that score above a threshold get forwarded to recruiters.

Key Factors ATS Systems Evaluate

  • Keyword matching: The most critical factor. ATS looks for exact and related keywords from the job description in your resume — job titles, technical skills, tools, certifications, and industry terms.
  • Section headers: Standard sections like "Experience," "Education," "Skills" help ATS parse your resume correctly. Creative headers like "My Journey" can confuse parsers.
  • Formatting: Simple, clean formatting works best. Avoid tables, columns, headers/footers, images, and graphics — most ATS can't read these.
  • File format: Submit .docx or .pdf unless specified otherwise. Plain text (.txt) is the safest but loses formatting.
  • Chronological order: List your most recent experience first. ATS and recruiters both prefer reverse-chronological format.

Tips to Beat the ATS

  • Mirror the job description: Use the exact phrases and terminology from the posting. If they say "project management," don't just write "managed projects."
  • Include a skills section: List both hard skills (Python, SQL, Figma) and soft skills (leadership, communication) that match the job.
  • Quantify achievements: Use numbers — "Increased revenue by 35%" or "Managed team of 12" — to stand out to both ATS and recruiters.
  • Spell out acronyms: Write "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)" so ATS catches both forms.
  • Use standard job titles: If your actual title was creative (e.g., "Growth Ninja"), add the standard equivalent in parentheses.
  • Tailor for each application: One-size-fits-all resumes score lower. Customize keywords for each job.

Common ATS Resume Mistakes

Avoid these pitfalls that cause ATS to misread or reject resumes: using images or icons for contact info, putting key information in headers or footers (ATS often skips these), using uncommon fonts, embedding text in graphics, and having no clear section structure. Stick to standard fonts, clean layouts, and plain text content for the best results.
